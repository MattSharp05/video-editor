# Source real meshes, build the rest procedurally

**Use real meshes where anatomy has to read as true, and build everything else in code.** The brain should be the Brainder "Brain for Blender" FreeSurfer pial surfaces, decimated and baked to GLB. Use NeuroMorpho SWC reconstructions for the neurons and Kenney's CC0 City Kit for the town. Build the sleeping man, the light ribbons and the dissolve effect procedurally in three.js r169, using recipes borrowed from three skill sets: **InstruktAI/TeleClaude `threejs-procedural-assets`** (lofted organic bodies, instanced scatter), **EL4CTEO/blender-skills** (headless Blender 5.2 sculpt substitutes, Geometry Nodes, web export) and **SummerEngine `vfx-dissolve`** (noise-threshold dissolve with an emissive rim). linegel's and scottstts' three.js packs add topology and particle-architecture rules. Text-to-3D and image-to-3D generators are not worth it here. The only ones with a CPU path, SF3D and TripoSR, give smooth, low-detail single-image meshes that would turn gyri to mush. The quality leaders (TRELLIS.2, Hunyuan3D-2.x) need 10–24 GB of NVIDIA VRAM. The Reel's licence load stays light: CC0 town, CC BY 4.0 neurons with three citations, and one decision for Matthew on the Brainder brain's share-alike (CC BY-SA 3.0) or the CC BY 4.0 BodyParts3D alternative. Everything below runs offline on CPU once its assets are downloaded. Per-frame cost under SwiftShader depends on fragment work and overdraw, not triangle count, so all geometry is built once and animation runs through uniforms. The repo's earlier note `lab/3d-skills-research.md` already covers the terrain, water and lighting skill packs, so they are not repeated here.

## Three skill sets supply recipes, and none of them models a brain

GitHub search turned up eight relevant SKILL.md sets. Only some of them work on this stack. **EL4CTEO/blender-skills** (743 stars, MIT) has 28 Blender 5.2 skills built for `blender -b`, with "67 headless Blender tests" ([README](https://github.com/EL4CTEO/blender-skills/blob/main/README.md)). Its most useful rule for a headless container is a warning: **"Sculpt Mode operators crash Blender in background mode (-b)"**. It replaces sculpting with object-level `sculpt_ops.py` operations instead: `remesh`, `inflate`, `detail=STR,SCALE` noise, `smooth` and `multires`, on top of a metaball/Skin-modifier blockout ([blender-sculpting](https://github.com/EL4CTEO/blender-skills/blob/main/skills/blender-sculpting/SKILL.md)). Its workflow recipe 7 exports web assets as meshopt + WebP, ≤30k tris and under 5 MB ([workflow-recipes](https://github.com/EL4CTEO/blender-skills/blob/main/skills/blender-workflow-recipes/SKILL.md)). **TeleClaude's `threejs-procedural-assets`** (GPL-3.0) is the most directly usable three.js skill. It bans "glue together spheres, cylinders and boxes" and prescribes a **lofted spline surface** for organic bodies: a spine curve, separate width and height profiles, caps formed by tapering the profiles to near zero, limbs as separate lofts sunk into the body, and 2–3 octaves of simplex noise at 0.5–2% of radius for skin. It gives concrete segment counts (100+ along the main axis, 48–64 around it) and says a 100k-triangle asset "renders fine" ([SKILL.md](https://github.com/InstruktAI/TeleClaude/blob/main/agents/global/skills/threejs-procedural-assets/SKILL.md), [geometry.md](https://github.com/InstruktAI/TeleClaude/blob/main/agents/global/skills/threejs-procedural-assets/references/geometry.md)). **SummerEngine `vfx-dissolve`** (MIT) is written in Godot shader language but carries over to any engine. It samples object-space FBM, discards below a threshold, and lights the rim with `smoothstep(threshold + edge_width, threshold, n)`, with defaults of edge width 0.06, emission 6 and noise scale 3. It also warns that view-space coordinates make the pattern "slide with the camera" ([SKILL.md](https://github.com/SummerEngine/summer/blob/main/plugins/claude/skills/vfx-dissolve/SKILL.md)).

The other packs are worth reading for their reasoning, but their code won't run here. **linegel/threejs-complete-set-of-skill** (ISC) and **scottstts/Threejs-Awesome-Graphics-Agent-Skills** (MIT, 976 stars) both target r185 WebGPU/TSL, and TSL won't compile on r169 WebGL2. Their transferable rules still apply. For sweeps, use parallel-transport frames, not Frenet frames ([linegel procedural-geometry](https://github.com/linegel/threejs-complete-set-of-skill/blob/main/skills/threejs-procedural-geometry/SKILL.md)). Write particles and trails as "pure functions of spawn data and time" ([linegel particles-trails](https://github.com/linegel/threejs-complete-set-of-skill/blob/main/skills/threejs-particles-trails-and-effects/SKILL.md)). Build towns with a footprint → extrude → inset grammar ([linegel buildings-and-cities](https://github.com/linegel/threejs-complete-set-of-skill/blob/main/skills/threejs-procedural-buildings-and-cities/SKILL.md)). Pool trail instances rather than allocating them per burst ([scottstts vfx](https://github.com/scottstts/Threejs-Awesome-Graphics-Agent-Skills/blob/main/skills/threejs-procedural-vfx/SKILL.md)). **ahujasid/blender-mcp** (33k stars, MIT) is popular but a poor fit for this container. It needs a running Blender with a socket add-on that "has no authentication or encryption", and its generator tools call paid cloud APIs ([README](https://github.com/ahujasid/blender-mcp/blob/main/README.md)). **BradyAJohnston/nodebpy** (GPL-3.0) builds Geometry Nodes trees as Python on top of the pip `bpy` wheel ([SKILL.md](https://github.com/BradyAJohnston/nodebpy/blob/main/skills/nodebpy/SKILL.md)), and `bpy` 5.2.3 ships a cp313 manylinux wheel that matches this container's Python 3.13 ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/pypi/bpy/json)). **img2obj** (MIT, 1.7k stars) has a review loop worth copying even if its pipeline is not used: build, then render a 2×2 sheet of three-quarter, side, back and front views and compare it against the reference ([SKILL.md](https://github.com/vinhhien112/img2obj/blob/main/skills/object-to-threejs-procedural/SKILL.md)).

No skill models a cortex, a sleeping human or a neuron. The one "brain-viz" skill found is an fMRI viewer that needs an external backend ([mercury brain-viz](https://github.com/AlexiosBluffMara/mercury/blob/main/skills/creative/brain-viz/SKILL.md)). The per-area recipes below therefore combine these skills' techniques; none of them comes ready-made. Licences matter for vendoring. Copying TeleClaude or nodebpy text into `.claude/skills/` would bring GPL-3.0 obligations with it, so re-implement their techniques in our own code and record the borrowing in `UPSTREAM.md` without copying files. Licences for the GitHub repos come from search metadata, because the repos API was blocked in this session.

## Real anatomy beats every procedural brain trick

**Brainder's "Brain for Blender" is the best-looking brain available**, and it is a direct download from this container. It contains real FreeSurfer pial surfaces from one subject, already cut into one OBJ per Desikan-Killiany, DKT or Destrieux region, plus a subcortical set: hippocampus, amygdala, thalamus, cerebellum, brainstem and corpus callosum ([Brainder](https://brainder.org/research/brain-for-blender/)). Measured in this container, `pial_Full_obj.tar.bz2` is 6.9 MB and holds **289k + 290k faces** for the two hemispheres. The subcortical archive adds 290k faces. The catch is the licence: **CC BY-SA 3.0** ([Brainder](https://brainder.org/research/brain-for-blender/)). The mesh's share-alike clearly carries over to adaptations of the mesh. Whether a Reel that shows the mesh counts as an adaptation that must itself be BY-SA is a grey area, and Matthew should decide it. The clean alternative is **BodyParts3D 4.0**, whose licence page now reads **CC BY 4.0**, with the credit line "BodyParts3D, © The Database Center for Life Science licensed under CC Attribution 4.0 International" ([licence](https://dbarchive.biosciencedbc.jp/en/bodyparts3d/lic.html)). Older releases said CC BY-SA 2.1 Japan, so take the `LATEST/` data and keep a copy of the licence page ([2011 README](https://dbarchive.biosciencedbc.jp/data/bodyparts3d/20110915/README_e.html)). Its parts lists include hemispheres, cerebellum, pons, hippocampus, amygdala, thalamus and 24 named gyri ([partof list](https://dbarchive.biosciencedbc.jp/data/bodyparts3d/LATEST/partof_parts_list_e.txt), [isa list](https://dbarchive.biosciencedbc.jp/data/bodyparts3d/LATEST/isa_parts_list_e.txt)). Its cortical detail has not been viewed yet, and the "99" archives are reduced meshes, so render a still before choosing it. NIH 3D's HRA "Brain, Male" GLB (141 Allen structures, CC BY 4.0) is a third option, but no direct download URL was confirmed ([NIH 3D 20960](https://3d.nih.gov/entries/20960?version=1)). Avoid FreeSurfer's fsaverage ("designed for research purposes only") and Allen Institute meshes (non-commercial without written permission) ([FreeSurfer licence](https://surfer.nmr.mgh.harvard.edu/fswiki/FreeSurferSoftwareLicense), [Allen terms](https://alleninstitute.org/terms-of-use/)).

The procedural brain is the fallback, and it has a direct precedent. prisoner849's "SYNAPSES" demo deforms a unit sphere with `ImprovedNoise`, carves the longitudinal fissure with `smoothstep(0.375, 0, abs(x))` and scales the axes to get the lobes. It draws the signal flows as fat lines on `CatmullRomCurve3` curves with selective bloom ([discourse](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/synapses-fat-lines-selective-bloom/35049)). Adding real-looking folds to that hull means displacing along the normal with **ridged, domain-warped noise**, or with a baked Gray-Scott "maze" reaction-diffusion texture ([colejd/Reaction-Diffusion-ThreeJS](https://www.github.com/colejd/Reaction-Diffusion-ThreeJS), [Reaction-Diffusion Playground](https://compmonks.github.io/reaction-diffusion-playground)). Sulci should be narrow and deep and gyri broad, with displacement around 3–6% of the radius, well above TeleClaude's 0.5–2% skin band. Even done well, this still reads as an illustrated brain. The Brainder surface has real anatomy that noise cannot fake, so the procedural version belongs in stylised or abstract shots, not the hero.

Under SwiftShader, the 579k-face pial pair is too heavy as is. Decimate each hemisphere to about **60–100k triangles** with trimesh or `gltf-transform simplify`. Quadric decimation keeps folds well at that level. Add the cerebellum and brainstem for a total near 200k. Bake one GLB with named nodes so a shot can call `getObjectByName('Left-Amygdala')`. The per-region DK OBJs come from the same surface, so their seams line up. Tinting the visual cortex for "dream images" or the amygdala for "emotion" then needs only a uniform per mesh. For light "traffic along the folds", store the fold value as a vertex attribute and render a moving emissive band masked to the crests. That costs one extra fragment term and carries no state from frame to frame ([procedural recipe source: three.js r169 examples](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/tree/r169/examples/jsm)).

## A sleeping man needs a silhouette, and a duvet hides the rest

Under a blanket the body barely reads, so spend the polygons on the head. There are three routes, ranked by believability against effort. **MakeHuman/MPFB exports are CC0**, and they can be sold ([MakeHuman FAQ](https://static.makehumancommunity.org/oldsite/faq/can_i_sell_models_created_with_makehuman.html), [MPFB FAQ](https://static.makehumancommunity.org/mpfb/faq/can_i_sell_models.html)), but they need the desktop app or Blender to export. They are the most realistic route, but someone has to produce the GLB outside this container or through a headless Blender 5.2 install. **Quaternius characters** fall under QAL v1.0: free commercial use with no credit, but the asset files must not be redistributed, so keep them out of the public repo ([Quaternius licence](https://quaternius.com/license.html)). They suit a stylised man posed in code by rotating bones. The **three.js LeePerrySmith head** (CC BY 3.0, a 405 KB GLB, fetched with curl here) is a real scan of a head only. It works for a close-up profile on a pillow, with the credit "3D Head Scan by Lee Perry-Smith, CC BY 3.0" ([licence file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mrdoob/three.js/r170/examples/models/gltf/LeePerrySmith/LeePerrySmith_License.txt)). Avoid the three.js Xbot until its licence is confirmed.

If the look should stay stylised and consistent with the procedural world, build it in code instead. TeleClaude's lofted body works for the torso: a horizontal spine curve, width and height profiles with landmarks (neck pinch, shoulders, ribs, waist, hips), and limbs as separate lofts sunk into it. Breathing comes from deforming the chest profile, following its "animate by deformation" rule ([geometry.md](https://github.com/InstruktAI/TeleClaude/blob/main/agents/global/skills/threejs-procedural-assets/references/geometry.md)). Organic heads are easier as an SDF. Combine ellipsoids for cranium and jaw, a round cone for the nose and smooth-subtracted eye sockets with polynomial smooth-min. Mesh the result once at load with three r169's CPU `MarchingCubes` (`setCell` + `update()`) or the MIT `surface-nets` package, then copy it into a static geometry ([MarchingCubes.js](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/r169/examples/jsm/objects/MarchingCubes.js), [surface-nets](https://www.npmjs.com/package/surface-nets)). r169's own source calls a 32³ field "pushing it", so a 96–128³ grid sampled in a plain JS loop is the right scale. Run it once behind `delayRender` and never per frame. The blanket is a dense `PlaneGeometry` lifted to the maximum of the mattress height and a blurred body heightfield, plus low-frequency ridged wrinkles, with a Gaussian-masked `uBreath` uniform near the chest. In Blender, EL4CTEO's metaball blockout → voxel remesh → inflate/smooth path, checked against its human-proportions reference, gives a cleaner head than an SDF does ([blender-sculpting](https://github.com/EL4CTEO/blender-skills/blob/main/skills/blender-sculpting/SKILL.md)). Whether `review_shots.py` previews work without a GPU is unverified, so plan on Cycles CPU or Workbench for checks.

## Kenney's CC0 kit covers the town; shaders do the glow

**Kenney City Kit (Suburban) 2.0 is CC0 and ships GLB files**. The OpenGameArt mirror (3.0 MB) was downloaded here, and its `License.txt` confirms CC0 with optional credit. kenney.nl itself returned 403 from this container ([OpenGameArt](https://opengameart.org/content/city-kit-suburban)). The buildings share one colour-map texture and weigh about 100–150 KB each, so a street of 30–60 houses loads straight through `GLTFLoader`. If the town needs to be built rather than loaded, use one merged house geometry (box + prism roof) as an `InstancedMesh`, with a per-instance seed in an `InstancedBufferAttribute`, `setColorAt` variation and 2–3 geometry variants. TeleClaude notes that 2–3 variants "beats one variant at 3× the count" ([geometry.md](https://github.com/InstruktAI/TeleClaude/blob/main/agents/global/skills/threejs-procedural-assets/references/geometry.md)). Lit windows cost almost nothing when they are computed in the fragment shader: a window grid in object space, hashed by seed and cell to decide lit or dark, with "lights going out at bedtime" driven by a `uLitFraction` uniform. Aldervale is a working three.js night town of this kind ([ojja/aldervale](https://github.com/ojja/aldervale)).

The tiny glowing town reuses the same instance buffers with a hologram material: additive blending, `transparent`, `depthWrite: false`, double-sided, a fresnel rim and sine scanlines ([Three.js Journey hologram lesson](https://threejs-journey.com/lessons/hologram-shader), [ektogamat holographic material](https://github.com/ektogamat/threejs-vanilla-holographic-material)). Its cost under SwiftShader comes from additive overdraw, and it stays cheap because the model is small on screen. Fake bloom with emissive values above 1 and additive halo sprites, rather than running several full-resolution blur passes.

## Ribbons and the edge-crumble are stateless shaders

Remotion's three.js integration drives animation from `useCurrentFrame()`, not `useFrame()`, and it recommends the `angle` GL backend, passed explicitly through `chromiumOptions` for SSR renders ([Remotion @remotion/three](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/three)). That forces every effect to be a pure function of the frame, which is exactly linegel's rule for trails. **Light ribbons** are camera-facing strips swept along `CatmullRomCurve3` curves, with an attribute `aT` and a moving window `smoothstep(head - len, head, aT)` in the fragment shader. The other option is `meshline@3.3.1` (MIT, peer `three >=0.137`) with an animated `dashOffset`, or r169's own `Line2`/`LineMaterial` ([npm meshline](https://npmjs.com/package/meshline), [r169 lines](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/tree/r169/examples/jsm/lines)). Avoid drei's `<Trail>`, `<Sparkles>` and `<Float>`, which sample wall-clock time or previous frames. Particle trails are the particle's closed-form path evaluated at `t − i·dt`. Curl noise (Bridson et al. 2007) gives divergence-free, fluid-looking paths, but only once the paths are precomputed into arrays, since advection carries state from frame to frame ([Bridson 2007](https://www.dneg.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/2007.pdf)).

For **"crumble from the edges"**, start from Summer's dissolve and add a boundary bias, so the dissolve front starts at the silhouette or rim and moves inward: `d = 0.7·noise + 0.3·edgeDistance`, discard when `d < uProgress`, and emit along the band just above it. Codrops' February 2025 tutorial uses the same structure: shader snippets injected into the existing material, an edge band of colour, and particles shown only near the dissolving edge ([Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2025/02/17/implementing-a-dissolve-effect-with-shaders-and-particles-in-three-js/)). The edge bias is this report's own extension; none of the sources covers it. Compute it per vertex as geodesic distance from the mesh boundary, or more cheaply as distance from a chosen plane or point. Crumbs come from `MeshSurfaceSampler` points, each with a birth value equal to its dissolve value, then a closed-form fall: `p + normal·age + curlPath(seed)·age − g·age²`. Inject it through `onBeforeCompile` with a `customProgramCacheKey`, or through `three-custom-shader-material` 6.x (MIT, peers admit r169 and R3F 8) ([npm CSM](https://npmjs.com/package/three-custom-shader-material)). Log the fragment shader once to confirm r169's chunk names. Keep FBM to 2–4 octaves, because SwiftShader's cost scales with pixels shaded.

Dependency versions need pinning. The current releases of drei (10.x), `@react-three/postprocessing` (3.x), lamina (1.2) and `three-bvh-csg` (0.0.18) require React 19, R3F 9 or three ≥0.170/0.179. Pin `drei@9.122.0`, `@react-three/postprocessing@2.19.x`, `three-bvh-csg@0.0.17` and `postprocessing` 6.37.x. Note that `postprocessing` is **Zlib**-licensed, and that its 6.36.x peer range excludes r169 ([npm postprocessing](https://www.npmjs.com/package/postprocessing), [npm drei](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@react-three/drei), [npm three-bvh-csg](https://www.npmjs.com/package/three-bvh-csg)).

## Real neurons arrive as SWC, and generators fail on CPU

**NeuroMorpho.Org is CC BY 4.0**, and its API and direct SWC URLs both work from this container, e.g. `dableFiles/wearne_hof/CNG%20version/cnic_001.CNG.swc`, a monkey prefrontal pyramidal cell ([terms](https://neuromorpho.org/useterm.jsp)). The licence requires three citations: the original paper, "NeuroMorpho.Org (RRID:SCR_002145)", and Tecuatl, Ljungquist & Ascoli (2024). Put them in the caption or a pinned comment. Prefer non-Allen archives, since it is unconfirmed whether Allen-derived neurons keep Allen's non-commercial terms. Each SWC row (`id type x y z radius parent`) maps to a tapered tube. Split the tree into unbranched chains, simplify each with Ramer-Douglas-Peucker, sweep it with 6–8 radial segments using the SWC radii (r169's `TubeGeometry` takes only a constant radius), and merge everything into one draw call. Janelia's SharkViewer already does SWC-in-three.js ([SharkViewer](https://www.github.com/JaneliaSciComp/SharkViewer)). For stylised or extra neurons, grow skeletons with space colonization (Runions et al. 2007) and taper them by a Rall-style radius rule ([Gustafsson tree generator](https://torstengustafsson.github.io/pages/projects/treegeneration)). Action potentials are an emissive Gaussian along a per-vertex `aPathDist`, and vesicles are an `InstancedMesh` on closed-form paths.

**Text-to-3D and image-to-3D are not viable for the hero assets.** **SF3D** is the only model with an official CPU backend (`SF3D_USE_CPU=1`), and it outputs a UV-textured GLB from one image ([Stability-AI/stable-fast-3d](https://github.com/Stability-AI/stable-fast-3d)). It falls under the Stability Community License (free below $1M revenue) and is gated on Hugging Face ([stability.ai/license](https://stability.ai/license)). **TripoSR** (MIT) also runs without a GPU, and its marching-cubes step has a CPU build ([TripoSR](https://github.com/VAST-AI-Research/TripoSR)). No primary source gives a CPU timing for either model, so a container benchmark comes first. **TRELLIS.2** needs a 24 GB NVIDIA GPU plus flash-attn, nvdiffrast and CuMesh ([microsoft/TRELLIS.2](https://github.com/microsoft/TRELLIS.2)). Hunyuan3D-2 took **45 minutes for shape alone** on an 8 GB GPU with CPU offload ([issue #373](https://github.com/Tencent-Hunyuan/Hunyuan3D-2/issues/373)). Its licence also excludes the EU, UK and South Korea and requires public outputs to be labelled "machine generated" ([LICENSE](https://github.com/Tencent-Hunyuan/Hunyuan3D-2/blob/main/LICENSE)). Shap-E, the only true text-to-3D model in reach, was 3% done after an hour on a laptop CPU ([Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/news/openai-shap-e-creates-3d-models)). A CPU text-to-image front end is fast: SD-Turbo produces a 512² image in 1.7–7.8 s on a 12-core i7 ([fastsdcpu](https://github.com/rupeshs/fastsdcpu/blob/main/Readme.md)). Its OpenVINO build, though, is marked non-commercial research only ([sd-turbo-openvino](https://huggingface.co/rupeshs/sd-turbo-openvino)). Hosted routes are tiny or conditional. ZeroGPU gives about 5 GPU-minutes a day on a free account ([HF ZeroGPU](https://huggingface.co/docs/hub/spaces-zerogpu)), and Meshy free outputs are CC BY 4.0 ([Meshy pricing](https://docs.meshy.ai/en/webapp/pricing)). Both also need Matthew's go-ahead under the repo's "nothing gets uploaded" rule. The only defensible use is SF3D on a small background prop, such as a pillow or a lamp.

## Conclusion

The weak areas look like a modelling problem, but they come down mostly to sourcing and shaders. Two of the five (brain and neurons) are solved by real scientific data under open licences that no procedural trick or CPU generator can match. Two (ribbons and the dissolve) need no meshes at all, only stateless shader functions of the frame, which suit Remotion's frame-addressed rendering better than any simulation. Only the sleeping man still calls for real modelling effort, and a duvet removes most of it. The open question is legal rather than technical: whether Brainder's share-alike reaches a published Reel. Matthew should settle it before the brain shots are locked, because switching to BodyParts3D afterwards means re-checking every brain close-up.

The research also shows where agent skills stand right now. The strongest three.js skills target a WebGPU/TSL stack this project doesn't use, and the strongest Blender skills assume Blender lives next to the agent. The most useful thing to fold back into `animated-video` is a set of rules rather than any single skill: real data before procedural fakes, everything computed from the frame, geometry built once, and multi-angle review sheets before a render.
