# Microwave 01: picture brainstorm (fresh agent, no production rules)

Ideas per line, each a different kind of picture. Every one says what we see and what happens to it.
A note on the "grows from the old frame" rule: each idea lists a possible **hand-off** (the object that
carries the eye into the next line) where one comes naturally. The tide comment applies to all of this:
something in frame should always be changing, travelling or transforming, never only arrows blinking on
and off.

---

## 1. "Most people think a microwave cooks food from the inside out."

- **Literal.** A bowl of soup in a warm kitchen, then a cutaway: a glowing orange core at the centre spreads
  outward ring by ring, like a sunrise inside the soup. A big question mark rises out of the steam.
  Hand-off: the question mark's dot drops back into the soup.
- **Everyday analogy.** A chocolate lava cake: a fork breaks it open and molten chocolate pours out of the
  middle. "That's what we picture." The cake is sitting on a microwave turntable.
- **Wacky.** A tiny chef with a hard hat climbs out of a trapdoor in the middle of a potato, holding a
  blowtorch, and starts heating it from the inside. He waves at the camera.
- **Personified.** A thought bubble over a crowd of cartoon people, all imagining the same thing: a
  meatball with a little campfire inside it. The bubbles merge into one big bubble.
- **Kid's drawing.** A crayon microwave with a crayon sun drawn inside the food, rays pointing outward.
  A red "?" scribble appears over it. Hand-off: the crayon sun gets crossed out and the camera pushes
  into the scribble.

## 2. "But actually, it heats the outside first. Then the heat creeps in."

- **Literal.** Thermal-camera view of a potato: the skin flushes red, then the red band slowly crawls
  inward, ring after ring, while the centre stays blue until last.
- **Everyday analogy.** A sunburn that starts on your skin, or a slice of toast getting butter on the
  outside first, then the butter soaking in. Better: a crowd at a stadium doing a "wave" from the outer
  rows to the centre seats.
- **Wacky.** The heat is a team of tiny orange gnomes who parachute onto the food's surface, then
  tunnel inward with shovels, slowly. The ones in front complain it's a long way down.
- **Change of scale.** A planet-sized meatball seen from orbit: the crust lights up like lava fields, and
  the glow seeps down through the layers like the earth's mantle in reverse.
- **Kid's drawing.** The lava cake from line 1 again, flipped: the frosting outside turns red, a little
  sad blue face in the middle waits, then turns orange and smiles. Hand-off: zoom past the cake to the
  microwave's back wall to find out why.

## 3. "Inside the oven, a part called a magnetron makes invisible waves."

- **Literal.** X-ray through the microwave's side panel: a copper cylinder with fins (the magnetron)
  hums, glows faintly, and pushes out a ripple that is invisible until we put on "wave goggles" and see it.
- **Everyday analogy.** A stone dropped into a pond: the magnetron is the stone, the ripples spread out
  across the oven. Or a speaker cone thumping out sound you can't see.
- **Personified.** The magnetron is a small grumpy drummer locked in the back of the box, hammering a
  drum so fast his arms blur. Every beat sends out a ring.
- **Wacky.** A tiny lighthouse in the corner of the microwave sweeps a beam around, except the beam is
  invisible until a cloud of flour is thrown in front of it.
- **Kid's drawing.** A box with a "magic wand" glued inside, shooting squiggly lines. The squiggles
  start out dotted (invisible) and fill in. Hand-off: one squiggle shoots out across the box and the
  camera rides on it.

## 4. "These microwaves bounce around the metal box. The holes in the door are too small to let them out."

- **Literal.** Ride along with one wave as it ricochets off the walls, ceiling and floor like a
  pinball, leaving a glowing trail that builds up a tangled web. Then it hits the door mesh and
  bounces off.
- **Everyday analogy.** A beach ball trying to get through a tennis net: the ball is far too big for the
  holes. Or a big dog trying to squeeze through a cat flap. Meanwhile light (a tiny marble) slips
  through, which is why you can still see your food.
- **Change of scale.** Shrink to the size of an ant standing on the door's mesh: the holes are manholes
  to us, but a wave as wide as a bus rolls up, can't fit, and squashes against them like a whale
  against a fence.
- **Personified.** Waves as a gang of bouncy rubber balls with faces, partying in a squash court. One
  tries to sneak out the door, gets stuck in a hole up to its waist, and is pulled back by its friends.
- **Wacky.** The microwave is a hall of mirrors at a funfair; the wave keeps seeing itself and bouncing
  off in panic.
- **Kid's drawing.** A box with zigzag arrows bouncing everywhere, and a door drawn as a fishing net
  with an angry wave tangled in it. Hand-off: zoom into the wave stuck in the mesh to see it swinging.

## 5. "Each wave swings back and forth billions of times every second."

- **Literal.** Freeze the wave on the door, zoom in until it is a single sine curve, then let it go: it
  flips up and down so fast it blurs into a ribbon. A counter spins up: 1... 1,000... 2,450,000,000.
- **Everyday analogy.** A jump rope being swung so fast the rope turns into a blurry tube. Or a
  hummingbird's wings, then "now imagine 50 million times faster".
- **Change of scale.** Slow time right down: a single second is stretched into a football field and the
  wave's swings are painted lines across it, billions of them, too many to count, so the field turns
  solid colour.
- **Personified.** The wave is a hyperactive metronome / a dancer doing the twist so fast she becomes a
  blur and sparks fly off her feet.
- **Wacky.** A clock's second hand moves one tick; in that tick, a stadium of tiny waves each do a
  thousand push-ups.
- **Kid's drawing.** A scribbled zigzag that gets scribbled harder and harder until the paper tears.
  Hand-off: the blur-ribbon slides down onto a water droplet sitting on the food.

## 6. "The water in your food is made of tiny molecules. Each one has a plus end and a minus end."

- **Literal.** Dive into a pea on the plate, through cell walls, into a drop of water, until it breaks
  up into countless H2O molecules: Mickey-Mouse-shaped, with a red "+" ears side and a blue "-" chin side.
- **Everyday analogy.** Each molecule is a tiny bar magnet, or a compass needle; or a battery with a
  plus and minus end. Show a pile of batteries tumbling out of the pea.
- **Personified.** Each water molecule is a little character with a mood: a happy "+" head-end and a
  grumpy "-" feet-end. Millions of them, sitting around, chatting.
- **Change of scale.** One drop of water swells to the size of a swimming pool and we see it is actually
  a ball pit, and every ball is a molecule wearing a "+" hat on one side.
- **Wacky.** The molecules are tiny sock-puppets: one ear says "+", the chin says "-".
- **Kid's drawing.** Circles with two smaller circles stuck on top (Mickey heads), "+" and "-"
  scribbled on them. Hand-off: the swinging wave from line 5 sweeps into the pool.

## 7. "The swinging wave tugs them one way, then the other. So they wiggle and bump into their neighbors."

- **Literal.** The wave passes through the crowd; each molecule's "+" end gets pulled up, then down,
  flipping back and forth in sync. They knock into each other like bumper cars.
- **Everyday analogy.** A crowded dance floor where the DJ keeps switching the beat: everyone turns left,
  turns right, and bumps elbows. Or compass needles spinning wildly as a magnet is waved over them.
- **Personified.** A ballroom of molecule-dancers; an invisible partner (the wave) grabs each one by the
  "+" hand and spins it; they start crashing into each other, spilling drinks.
- **Wacky.** A field of sunflowers that are actually magnets; a giant hand sweeps a magnet over them back
  and forth and they whip around, slapping their neighbours.
- **Change of scale.** A packed subway car (the food) where the train keeps braking and lurching:
  passengers (molecules) sway one way, then the other, and bash into each other.
- **Kid's drawing.** Lines of stick figures holding hands, swinging arms, arrows going both ways and
  "BONK!" stars where heads meet. Hand-off: the "BONK" stars start to glow.

## 8. "All that bumping makes heat."

- **Literal.** The bump-sparks multiply and colour the whole crowd from blue to red; steam lifts off the
  top of the frame.
- **Everyday analogy.** Rub your hands together fast on a cold day: warmth. Show a pair of hands
  rubbing, then reveal they are made of tiny molecules.
- **Personified.** The molecule-dancers are sweating, fanning themselves, taking off jackets; one
  pours a bottle of water over its head (it is water, so that's a joke).
- **Wacky.** Each bump pops like popcorn and the popcorn is little flames. Or: a mosh pit that makes a
  thermometer burst.
- **Change of scale.** Zoom out from the hot crowd: it is the edge of the soup, now steaming in the bowl.
- **Kid's drawing.** The "BONK" stars from line 7 turn into wavy heat lines drawn in red crayon.
  Hand-off: the heat lines rise and we pull back out to the potato.

## 9. "The waves only reach a little way into the food, so the outside gets hot first."

- **Literal.** Cross-section of the potato: waves dive in about a couple of centimetres, fade out like a
  torch beam in fog, and the outer ring glows red while the core stays blue.
- **Everyday analogy.** Sunlight in the sea: the top of the water is bright and warm, deeper down it gets
  dark. Or a garden hose sprinkler that only wets the edge of the lawn.
- **Personified.** The waves are swimmers diving into the food; they swim a few strokes in, run out of
  breath and pop back up. The water molecules at the edge throw a party; the ones in the middle are
  left waiting, cold and bored.
- **Wacky.** Waves knock on the potato's door; only the people living in the outer rooms open it. The
  centre rooms get the news later, passed hand to hand (that's the heat creeping in from line 2).
- **Change of scale.** The meatball-planet from line 2 again: the waves are meteors that only crater the
  surface. The callback closes the loop.
- **Kid's drawing.** A potato with a red outline crayoned thick and a blue middle with a sad face.
  Hand-off: pull up and out until the food is sitting on the turntable.

## 10. "Some spots get more waves than others. That's why your plate spins."

- **Literal.** Top-down view of the oven floor; the bouncing waves stack up into a pattern of hot spots
  and cold spots (a glowing polka-dot map). A plate of cheese sits still: one corner bubbles, one stays
  frozen. Then the turntable spins and every bit of food passes through the hot spots.
- **Everyday analogy.** Roasting marshmallows over a campfire: you turn the stick so every side gets
  golden. Or a rotisserie chicken. Or a merry-go-round where each child only gets wet when passing
  under the fountain.
- **Personified.** The food is a sunbather on a lounger, turning over so it doesn't burn on one side.
- **Wacky.** The plate is a DJ's turntable; the food scratches the record as it spins through the hot
  spots, each one making a "ding".
- **Classic visible proof.** A chocolate bar on a non-spinning plate melts in dots exactly at the hot
  spots, polka-dot puddles (real experiment, makes the pattern visible).
- **Kid's drawing.** A spinning plate with a swirl of "whee!" lines and the food smiling, while a
  plate that doesn't spin has one half on fire and the other half frozen with icicles.
  Hand-off: the spinning plate spins into a whirl that becomes the summary loop.

## 11. "So waves wiggle the water, the bumping makes heat, and your leftovers get hot."

- **Literal (one-shot recap).** One continuous zoom-out chain: the wave (line 5) grows into the jiggling
  molecules (line 7), whose bumping glows into heat (line 8), which pulls back into steaming leftovers on
  the spinning plate (line 10), and finally out through the door mesh to the person pulling it out.
- **Everyday analogy.** A Rube Goldberg machine: magnetron bonks a ball, ball bounces, shakes a box of
  dancing water molecules, they light a match, the match warms a plate of pasta. Ding.
- **Personified.** The whole cast takes a bow: grumpy drummer magnetron, bouncy waves, sweaty water
  molecules, the sunbathing food on the turntable. The microwave goes "ding" and the door opens.
- **Wacky.** The leftovers sit up, put on sunglasses, and say "I'm hot" as steam rises.
- **Change of scale.** Zoom out from a single water molecule all the way out to the kitchen, the house,
  the street, in one long pull, and the "ding" echoes over the whole town.
- **Kid's drawing.** A comic strip with three panels drawn in crayon (wave, wiggle, warm) that folds into
  a plate of steaming spaghetti on the last beat.
