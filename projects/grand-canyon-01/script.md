# Grand Canyon 01: script v0.1 (draft for Matthew)

**Idea:** A river cut the Grand Canyon a mile deep in about six million years, grinding with the sand it carries,
through rock up to almost two billion years old. **Goal:** reach (assumed; no goal named).
**Format:** Instagram Reel 9:16, narrated explainer, one continuous shot (lab principles), in the manner of the
"How does sand think?" reference (structure only, no wording borrowed).
**Runtime:** about 63–70 s (208 words; the three picked voices measured 3.0–3.5 words/s in the picker).
**Voice:** one of the three picked in the Voice Picker (open question below).

## The chain (each part answers the problem the one before created)
1. Hook: the rock is ~2 billion years old, the canyon ~6 million. Problem: how can a river cut a mile, that fast?
2. Abrasion: the river cuts with the sand and gravel it carries. Problem: sand only cuts when the water is fast.
3. Uplift: the land had been pushed up thousands of feet, so once the river found the sea it had a long way to fall.
4. Layers: cutting down opened a stack of old worlds; hard layers make cliffs, soft make slopes (the staircase).
5. Width: the river only cuts down; rain, flash floods and rockfall pull the walls apart.
6. Recap through every part, back to the rim; the canyon isn't finished.

## Alternative hooks (each with its own second sentence)
1. "The Grand Canyon was cut with sand." / "Not by wind: by the sand the Colorado River carries, scraping a mile down into rock."
2. "Stand on the rim of the Grand Canyon and you're looking down through almost two billion years of rock." / "And one river cut through all of it in about six million."

## Visual route (proposal; animated-video owns the timeline)
- **One continuous world, travelling in scale:** grain → riverbed → gorge → whole canyon → plateau block → back to the grain.
  Every verb gets an event on its word (scrapes, pushed up, cut, crumble, flash floods, pulling apart).
- **Real terrain:** the canyon built from public-domain USGS elevation data (3DEP) in three.js, with the rock layers
  as bands of colour on the walls. The uplift and cutting beats use a block diagram (a slab of layered land cut open,
  the way geologists draw it) that grows out of the real terrain, so there is no cut between them.
- **Macro beats** (the rock face, the sand grain) as close-up 3D materials; labelled "Magnified illustration".
- **Look:** a palette from the canyon itself (Kaibab cream, Redwall red, schist near-black, river green), captions at
  the top with the key word lifted (lab principle 3). Not Trip's design system: this is a different channel.
- **Honesty labels:** "Magnified illustration", "Timeline compressed", "Not to scale" where they apply.

## Draft caption
How the Grand Canyon formed, in a minute: the Colorado Plateau was lifted thousands of feet, the Colorado River found
its way to the sea about 5–6 million years ago and cut down through it with the sand and gravel it carries, and rain,
flash floods and rockfall widened it. Some geologists argue parts of the canyon are much older; the 5–6 million year
age is the National Park Service's figure for the canyon as one through-flowing river. Rock at the bottom: about
1.8 billion years old. Illustrations not to scale.
