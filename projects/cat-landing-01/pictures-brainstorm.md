# Cat landing 01: picture brainstorm (fresh agent, no production rules)

Source: `lines.txt` (11 lines, ~52 s, 9:16). Each idea says what we see and what happens to it.
Kind tags: **literal**, **analogy**, **wacky**, **scale**, **personified**, **kid drawing**.
Keep the creator's rules in mind: no cuts (each frame grows out of the last one), one point of
focus that leads into the next frame, no black "presentation" background, and something must
change in every frame (no arrows popping on and off a still picture).

A possible through-line for the whole film: **one orange cat, one fall, from the top of the
frame to the floor.** The camera falls with her. Every explanation happens in the air around her,
in slow motion or with time frozen, and the floor gets closer as the video goes on. The landing is
the payoff at the end.

---

## 1. "Most people think a cat's tail flips it over when it falls."

- **Literal.** A cat drops upside down from a windowsill. Her tail whirls like a propeller and she flips. A big "?" stamp lands on the tail. Then the film freezes in the air. This is the myth, so it's played a little too neatly, like a cartoon.
- **Analogy (the myth as a gadget).** The tail turns into a wind-up key. A hand winds it and the cat spins like a clockwork toy. "Most people think" is spoken over this toy version, so the myth already looks silly.
- **Personified.** A crowd of thought bubbles rises from the bottom of the frame ("it's the tail!", "tail, obviously"). Each bubble has a little doodled tail spinning inside it. The bubbles drift up and gather around the falling cat's real tail, which looks proud.
- **Wacky.** The tail is a helicopter rotor and the cat is the helicopter: tail spinning, *wop-wop-wop* sound, a pilot's headset on the cat. The camera pushes into the spinning tail, and that blur carries us into line 2.
- **Kid drawing.** A crayon cat with a curly arrow drawn round its tail, labelled "TAIL = FLIP". It looks confident and wrong. This sets up the "But actually".

## 2. "But actually, cats with no tail can do it too."

- **Literal (the continuation).** The tail-propeller blur clears and the tail is gone: it's a Manx cat (a breed with no tail), still upside down in the air. It flips anyway and lands on the same beat as line 1's cat. The tail in the "?" stamp from line 1 falls off and spins away out of frame.
- **Wacky (removing the tail).** A big pair of scissors snips the cartoon tail off, or the tail is unzipped like a costume piece and hung on a coat hook. The cat flips anyway and shrugs mid-air.
- **Personified.** The tail is a little character that has been taking the credit. It wears a medal, then gets a pink slip and walks off the side of the frame with its box of belongings. The cat flips without it, which shows the tail never mattered.
- **Change of scale (proof by lineup).** The cat multiplies into a falling row: long tail, stubby tail, no tail (Manx). All three flip at the same moment, like synchronised swimmers. The no-tail cat's spin pulls the camera in close for line 3.
- **Kid drawing.** The crayon "TAIL = FLIP" arrow from line 1 gets scribbled out. The kid redraws the cat with no tail and adds the same curly arrow round its middle instead. That middle arrow foreshadows line 5.

## 3. "First, the cat's inner ear and eyes tell it which way is up."

- **Literal (inside the head).** The camera zooms into the cat's ear while she's still upside down. Inside, three looping fluid tubes (the semicircular canals, the ear's motion sensors) slosh like a water level. A gold arrow reading "UP" swings round and points at the sky. At the same moment her pupils roll to find the horizon.
- **Analogy (the spirit level).** A builder's spirit level sits inside the cat's head. Its bubble slides to one end, and the cat's whole body twitches toward it. It's an everyday object almost everyone has tilted.
- **Analogy (the phone).** A phone in the cat's head flips its screen from portrait to landscape, the way phones auto-rotate. It shows "↑ this way" and the cat's eyes follow the arrow.
- **Personified (control room).** Inside the head is a tiny mission-control room. The "Ear" operator at a sloshing fluid gauge shouts "We're upside down!" The "Eyes" operator at two periscopes shouts "Floor's up there!" The captain slams a big red button labelled TURN. Then we pull back out through the ear, and the cat is still frozen upside down.
- **Change of scale.** We dive into the ear canal and surf the fluid wave like a tiny surfer. The wave tips toward "down", and the camera rides it back out of the ear into the open air, which leads into line 4.
- **Kid drawing.** A cat face with a tiny compass drawn in its ear and an arrow drawn from its eye to a crayon sun, labelled "THIS WAY UP".

## 4. "But in the air, there's nothing to push on. So how can it turn?"

- **Literal.** The camera pulls far back. The cat hangs in a huge, empty sky with nothing touching her paws. She paddles her legs and nothing happens. The space around her feels conspicuously empty.
- **Analogy (the office chair).** A person sits on a spinning office chair with their feet off the floor and tries to turn round. They wiggle and get nowhere. Their feet reach for the floor and can't touch it. Pairing this with the cat makes "nothing to push on" a feeling, not just a fact.
- **Analogy (the astronaut).** An astronaut floats in the space station, upside down, flailing, trying to face the camera. The cat floats next to them in a spacesuit helmet. Both look at each other, then at the viewer. The astronaut's floating pen drifts to the centre of the frame and becomes the focal point that leads into line 5.
- **Wacky.** The cat tries to push off things that aren't there. She reaches out for a wall, and the wall is a chalk outline that crumbles. She tries a ladder, and it's drawn in dotted lines that vanish. The handholds keep vanishing, so you feel that there is nothing to grab.
- **Personified.** A little "PUSH HERE" sign floats nearby with a hand-drawn arrow, and the cat lunges for it. The sign shrugs and flies away. A huge question mark forms out of the cat's own curling body.
- **Kid drawing.** The cat floats in the middle of a blank page. The kid draws a floor under it, then rubs the floor out with an eraser.

## 5. "The cat bends in the middle, so each half can turn on its own."

- **Literal (the bend).** In slow motion, the cat's spine curves into a "V" or "C" at the waist. The front half and back half light up in two colours: the front half cobalt, the back half orange. The waist glows like a joint.
- **Analogy (the twist bottle).** The cat becomes a two-part object: a pepper grinder, or a peppermint wrapper twisted in the middle. Each end can turn while the other holds still. The two-colour cat morphs into the grinder and back.
- **Analogy (two cans on a hinge).** The cat is redrawn as two soup cans joined by a bendy straw. Each can rotates on its own axis while the straw stays bent. It's the simplest model of a cat-righting reflex, and it still reads as a cat (one can has ears and the other has a tail stub).
- **Personified.** The front half and back half are two roommates sharing one body. The front half has eyes and says "I'll go first." The back half grumbles "Fine, I'll wait." A tiny doorway opens at the waist, as if each half can now act alone.
- **Wacky.** The cat comes apart at the waist into two halves held together by a bungee cord, or a slinky. Each half spins freely on its end of the slinky.
- **Kid drawing.** A cat drawn as two scribbled circles (head end and bottom end) joined by a bendy line. A spinny-arrow is drawn on each circle.

## 6. "It tucks its front legs in, so the front spins fast. Its back legs stick out, so the back barely turns."

- **Literal (with time).** The front paws fold tight under the chest and the front half whips around fast, leaving a little motion blur. The back legs splay wide and the back half barely moves, turning a few degrees the other way. A spoken "fast" and "slow" match the two speeds.
- **Analogy (the figure skater).** A figure skater spins in a spot of light right next to the cat's front half. When she pulls her arms in she becomes a blur. The cat's front tucks at the very same moment and spins just as fast. Her spinning sparkles fade, but the cat's front keeps the speed. This is the classic way to show tuck-in = fast.
- **Analogy (the office chair, payoff).** The person from line 4 is back on the office chair, now holding two heavy books. Arms in: they spin fast. Arms out: they slow right down. Use the same chair and a different discovery.
- **Wacky (the weight).** Each half of the cat gets a dial labelled "how hard to turn". The tucked front half's dial reads "featherweight"; the splayed back half shows "sumo". A tiny sumo wrestler appears clinging to the back legs to show the back half is hard to turn.
- **Change of scale.** Seen from the cat's tail, looking up her body like a corridor. The front half spins like a drill bit at the far end; the back half near us hardly moves. The shot's depth makes the speed difference obvious.
- **Personified.** The front legs hug each other ("Tuck!") and the front half twirls like a ballerina. The back legs stick out stiff like a starfish and grumble as they get dragged only a little.
- **Kid drawing.** Front half: lots of curly speed lines. Back half: one tiny short arrow. Crayon labels "ZOOM" and "meh".

## 7. "Then it swaps. Back legs in, front legs out, and the back half catches up."

- **Literal (mirror of line 6).** The legs trade poses: the front paws reach out wide toward the floor, and the back legs fold in. Now the back half whips round to line up with the front, and the cat is right-side up. The two halves' colours meet and blend into one at the waist, which is a visual "click".
- **Analogy (the skater, part 2).** The figure skater from line 6 throws her arms out and slows to a stop, facing the camera. A second skater, hugging their arms in, spins up beside her and stops next to her, perfectly in line. Two skaters on one line form a matching pair.
- **Analogy (screwing on a lid).** The cat becomes a jar and lid. The lid (front) turns first, then the jar (back) catches up and they lock with a *click*. This uses the twist-bottle image from line 5 again.
- **Personified.** The back half (the grumpy roommate from line 5) says "My turn!" and spins to catch up with the front half. They high-five at the waist. Both halves now face the floor together.
- **Wacky.** The cat is a combination lock. The front half has already been dialled to the right number. Now the back half's dial spins round and *click*: unlocked, with paws facing down.
- **Kid drawing.** The kid draws the "ZOOM" and "meh" labels again, but swapped: now the back says ZOOM and the front says meh. A big smiley face appears when both halves point the same way.

## 8. "It can all happen in less than half a second."

- **Literal (real speed).** Everything from lines 5–7 plays back at real speed in a blink: a flip that's almost too fast to see. Then a stopwatch hand ticks to 0.3 s and stops. The slow-motion explanation and the real-time blink appear on top of each other, which shows how much happened in so little time.
- **Analogy (one blink).** A giant human eye opens and closes. In the gap of that single blink, the whole flip happens, reflected in the pupil. A blink takes about a third of a second, so the comparison is fair and instantly felt.
- **Wacky (racing the snap).** A finger snaps. The cat has already flipped before the snap sound lands. Or a dropped piece of toast is still mid-air when the cat is already standing on the floor watching it.
- **Change of scale.** The timeline of the fall stretches across the frame like a long ruler. The whole twisting sequence is squeezed into a narrow sliver at the start of it, labelled "< 0.5 s". The rest of the ruler is just falling.
- **Personified.** The two halves of the cat shout "Done!" before the narrator finishes saying "half a second". The narrator's words pile up and bump into the cat's back.
- **Kid drawing.** A crayon clock with a tiny slice coloured in, and the word "FAST!!" drawn with too many exclamation marks.

## 9. "Then it spreads and bends its legs to land softly."

- **Literal.** The floor rushes up. All four legs spread like a table, the joints bend deep, and the body sinks. Paw pads squash and spread. The camera, which has been falling all this time, lands with her and bounces softly.
- **Analogy (the parachute).** As she spreads her legs, her loose belly skin stretches into a little kite or parachute shape (cats do this: the "flying squirrel" pose). The fall visibly slows just before landing.
- **Analogy (knees, like us).** A person jumps off a wall twice. First they land with stiff legs: *thunk*, and a crack in the floor. Then they land bending their knees: a soft *pat*. The cat lands next to the second jumper and sinks into the same soft squat.
- **Wacky (shock absorbers).** Her four legs become car shock absorbers or springs, compressing with a *boing*. Or her four paws become four marshmallows and squish flat on landing.
- **Change of scale.** A close-up of one paw pad hitting the floor in extreme slow motion. The pad spreads like a pancake, and little "squish" ripples run through it, like a beanbag landing.
- **Kid drawing.** A cat drawn as a table with four bendy legs, with "soft!" written underneath and a little cloud of dust.

## 10. "But cats can still get badly hurt. So never drop a cat to test this."

- **Literal (the warning, without showing harm).** Right after the soft landing, the frame pulls back to show a tall apartment building the cat could have fallen from. A paw-print "warning" sign rises up in the foreground. Keep the cat safe and calm in the frame: we show the risk, never an injury.
- **Personified (the cat says no).** The cat looks straight at the camera and holds up a paw like a stop sign. A little caption bubble from the cat: "Please don't." She folds her arms. It's a firm warning that still has a smile.
- **Analogy (crash-test dummy).** A cardboard cut-out "test cat" is shown being lifted up by a hand. A big red "NO" stamp slams down on the hand, which drops the cut-out and backs away.
- **Wacky.** Someone holds a cat out over a balcony, and the cat swiftly unfolds a tiny lawyer's briefcase and hands over a "cease and desist" letter. The person slowly puts the cat back down.
- **Kid drawing.** A crayon hand holding a cat, crossed out with a thick red X, and "NO!" written large. Below it, a heart drawn round a cat sitting safely on the floor.

## 11. "So the cat bends, tucks, twists, swaps, and lands on four paws."

- **Literal (recap).** The whole flip replays once more at a medium speed, and each step gets a single word that pops up next to the matching moment: bend, tuck, twist, swap, land. The words stay in the frame and stack up, so the last frame is the cat standing on the floor with the five words around it.
- **Analogy (dance steps).** The five steps become a dance move with footprints on the floor, like a ballroom dance chart. The cat steps through them in rhythm and lands on the final beat. The narrator's five verbs match the five beats.
- **Wacky (the cat as gymnast).** The cat performs the flip as an Olympic gymnast, landing with her front paws raised. A judges' table holds up scorecards: 10, 10, 10, 10. One judge is a dog, who holds up a 9.
- **Personified (curtain call).** All the characters from earlier come back for a bow: the tail with its box of belongings, the two roommate halves, the figure skater, the ear's mission-control crew. They line up beside the cat, who sits in the middle washing a paw as if nothing happened.
- **Kid drawing.** The whole fall as a five-panel crayon comic strip across the frame, one panel per verb. The last panel is a cat standing on four paws, smiling, with "THE END" written underneath.
- **Change of scale (loop ending).** The camera pulls back from the cat standing on the floor. We see the windowsill from line 1, and the cat jumps back up onto it. The last frame is the same as the first, so the video loops.
