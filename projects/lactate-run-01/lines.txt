Most people think lactic acid is waste that makes your legs burn.
But actually, it's fuel.
When you run, your muscles break down sugar to make energy.
At an easy pace, tiny engines in your muscles burn it all the way.
But when you run hard, sugar breaks down super fast. The tiny engines can't keep up.
So the extra turns into lactate, and it spills into your blood.
Your heart, your brain, and other muscles grab it and burn it again.
So why do your legs burn? Hard running also makes acid and other leftovers. Those help slow you down.
Run fast enough, and lactate piles up faster than you can use it. That pace is your lactate threshold.
Training pushes that line, because you grow more tiny engines.
So lactate isn't trash. It's backup fuel, and fit runners burn even more of it.
