Most people think noise cancelling headphones just block sound.
But actually, they make more sound. They play an upside down copy of the noise.
Sound is air being pushed and pulled, over and over, like a wave.
Tiny microphones on your headphones listen to the noise around you.
A little chip makes a flipped copy. Where the noise pushes, the copy pulls.
The speaker plays that copy along with your music.
When a push meets a pull, they cancel out. Most of the hum disappears right at your ear.
The chip knows which sound is your music, so it leaves your music alone.
It works best on steady hums, like a plane engine. Sudden sounds, like voices, are harder.
The soft cushions help block those.
So your headphones listen, flip the noise, and fight sound with sound.
