# Noise cancel 01: picture brainstorm (fresh agent, no production rules)

Each idea: **what we see**, then **what happens to it**. Kind in brackets. Where an idea
naturally hands off to the next line (one focus point growing into the next frame), that is
noted as "grows into".

---

## 1. "Most people think noise cancelling headphones just block sound."

1. [Literal] A pair of headphones on a head in a busy street; noise squiggles fly at the ear
   cups and bounce off like hail on an umbrella. The squiggles ping away, the person looks
   smug. Then one squiggle stops mid-air and turns to look at the camera, puzzled.
2. [Analogy] The ear cups are two castle gates; sound arrives as a marching army of little
   drum-beating soldiers. The gates slam, soldiers pile up against them, cartoon-style. A
   thought bubble labelled "what you think" frames the whole castle, then the bubble pops.
3. [Wacky] Someone stuffs a whole sofa cushion, a mattress and a sleeping cat into each ear
   cup to "block sound". The headphones swell to absurd size; the head disappears inside
   them. A tiny "nope" stamp lands on it.
4. [Kid drawing] Crayon headphones with a brick wall drawn inside each cup, and noise drawn as
   angry zigzag monsters bonking their heads on the wall. A big red question mark gets
   scribbled over the wall.
5. [Personified] The headphones as a bouncer at a club door, arms crossed, turning noises
   away ("not on the list"). A sneaky hum ducks under his arm anyway; the bouncer double-
   takes. Grows into: the camera follows that hum into the ear cup.

## 2. "But actually, they make more sound. They play an upside down copy of the noise."

1. [Literal] Inside the ear cup, a speaker cone fires out a second wave: the noise's wave,
   but flipped vertically, its peaks where the noise's troughs are. Rendered as the same
   squiggle doing a backflip and landing upside down.
2. [Analogy] The noise is a person walking along a line; the copy is their reflection in a
   puddle, walking upside down beneath them, step for step. The puddle line becomes the
   zero line of a waveform.
3. [Wacky] The bouncer from line 1 gives up and instead hires an exact twin of the noise,
   then hangs it upside down by its ankles like a bat. The upside-down twin starts singing
   the same tune.
4. [Change of scale] Zoom from the head into the ear cup until the speaker is a stadium-size
   loudspeaker; out of it marches a giant upside-down copy of the hum, waving a "MORE SOUND"
   banner. Counter-intuitive surprise sells the "make more sound" twist.
5. [Kid drawing] A kid draws the noise as a wiggly line, then flips the paper over and traces
   it through from the back, holding both up to the light so the two lines show mirror
   images.
6. [Personified] The noise is a grumpy rain cloud raining "BZZZ". The headphones produce a
   second cloud, identical but upside down, raining upward. They glare at each other.
   Grows into: the camera dives into the cloud and finds it is made of air.

## 3. "Sound is air being pushed and pulled, over and over, like a wave."

1. [Literal] A field of dots (air molecules) between a speaker and an ear. The cone pushes:
   dots bunch up into a dense band; the cone pulls: the band thins. Dense/thin bands travel
   toward the ear; a waveform draws itself above the dots, tracking the density.
2. [Analogy] A stadium crowd doing the wave: nobody leaves their seat, but the wave runs
   around the stadium. Each fan is an air molecule; standing = pushed, sitting = pulled.
3. [Analogy] A long Slinky stretched across the frame; a hand shoves one end back and forth,
   and squished coils ripple along to the far end, where they tap an eardrum.
4. [Wacky] The air is a crowd of tiny people in a packed train carriage. Every time the
   train jolts (push), they squash into the doors; jolt back (pull), they stretch out.
   The jolts pass carriage to carriage down to the ear station.
5. [Change of scale] Zoom down into the air near the ear until molecules are bouncy balls
   the size of beach balls; the push is a giant hand slamming them together, the pull a
   giant vacuum hose sucking them apart. Then zoom out: the beach-ball crowd, seen from far
   away, is the waveform.
6. [Kid drawing] Crayon dots that a kid's finger squishes together and spreads apart, with
   "PUSH" and "PULL" written in wobbly letters; the finger keeps going "over and over"
   faster until the dots blur into a wavy line. Grows into: the wavy line flows toward the
   headphones and hits a tiny microphone.

## 4. "Tiny microphones on your headphones listen to the noise around you."

1. [Literal] The camera rides the incoming wave to the outside of the ear cup, where a
   pinhole microphone sits. The wave enters the pinhole and the mic's diaphragm wiggles in
   time with it, drawing a live trace.
2. [Personified] The microphone is a little character with one giant ear, pressed against the
   cup like a spy at a door, notepad in hand, scribbling down the noise's squiggle stroke by
   stroke.
3. [Analogy] A tiny court stenographer sitting on the headphone band, typing every bump of the
   wave onto a paper ribbon that feeds into the cup.
4. [Change of scale] Shrink until the mic is the size of a satellite dish on a hilltop; the
   plane engine's hum rolls in like weather over the hills and the dish swivels to catch it.
5. [Wacky] The headphones sprout dozens of googly-eyed ears on stalks, like a snail's eye
   stalks, all turning toward the noise source. Grows into: the ears hand the noise inward
   to the chip.
6. [Kid drawing] A headphone with a little smiley face drawn on, hand cupped to its "ear",
   with a speech-bubble of zigzags being collected into a jar labelled "noise".

## 5. "A little chip makes a flipped copy. Where the noise pushes, the copy pulls."

1. [Literal] Inside the cup, a chip with pins; the noise trace flows into one side and comes
   out the other side as its mirror. Hold the two traces against each other: every peak of
   one lines up with a trough of the other, with dots below showing bunching vs thinning.
2. [Personified] The chip is a tiny chef with a pancake flipper. The noise wave arrives on a
   conveyor belt; the chef flips each slice of it upside down at lightning speed and slides
   it on. The chef never misses a beat, flipping faster as the hum speeds up.
3. [Analogy] Two kids on a seesaw: when the noise kid goes up, the copy kid goes down, always
   opposite. The seesaw plank becomes the zero line.
4. [Analogy] Tug of war on the eardrum: the noise pushes the eardrum in; at the same instant
   the copy grabs it from the other side and pulls it out. The eardrum stays perfectly
   still in the middle.
5. [Wacky] A photocopier inside the headphones prints the noise squiggle, but the paper comes
   out upside down every time, and a little copier character grins: "that's a feature".
6. [Kid drawing] Two hands drawn in crayon: one labelled "noise" pushing a balloon, the other
   labelled "copy" pulling it back by the string. Grows into: the copy trace gets handed to
   the speaker.

## 6. "The speaker plays that copy along with your music."

1. [Literal] The speaker cone in the cup moves with two things layered: the slow sway of a
   song's waveform plus the flipped hum riding on top. The two waves merge into one
   combined wiggle going out of the cone.
2. [Analogy] A DJ at a two-deck mixer: deck one spins "your music" (colourful record), deck
   two spins a plain grey record labelled "anti-hum". Both faders go up; the speaker plays
   them together.
3. [Analogy] A bus leaving the speaker with your music sitting in the comfy seats and the
   flipped copy riding along as a stowaway on the roof. Both arrive at the ear together.
4. [Personified] The music is a singer on stage; the copy is a backup singer standing behind,
   upside down (hanging from the lighting rig), singing the noise's tune in reverse
   harmony. The audience doesn't notice the backup singer at all.
5. [Wacky] The speaker cone is a trampoline; a music-note character and an upside-down hum
   character bounce on it together, launching toward the eardrum side by side.
   Grows into: they fly out and meet the incoming noise head-on.

## 7. "When a push meets a pull, they cancel out. Most of the hum disappears right at your ear."

1. [Literal] Noise wave from outside and the flipped copy from the speaker meet at the
   eardrum; add them point by point and the result flattens to a near-straight line. The
   air dots near the ear stop bunching and thinning and settle into evenly spaced calm.
2. [Analogy] Two ocean waves heading at each other, one a crest, one a trough of the same
   size; they collide and the sea goes glass-flat, a rubber duck that was bobbing suddenly
   sits still.
3. [Analogy] A hole and a pile of sand the same size: someone shovels the pile into the hole
   and the ground ends up perfectly flat. The pile is a push, the hole is a pull.
4. [Personified] The noise character and its upside-down twin run at each other, high-five
   at the eardrum, and both vanish in a soft puff, leaving a "shh" curl of smoke.
5. [Wacky] The hum is a buzzing fly; the copy is an anti-fly (same fly, upside down). They
   meet at the ear, cancel, and only a tiny leftover buzz remains ("most of the hum"), a
   leftover fly-wing floating down.
6. [Change of scale] Pull way back: an aircraft cabin full of passengers, each wrapped in a
   small bubble of glass-flat calm around their ears while the cabin air outside the
   bubbles still shudders with the hum. Grows into: the camera finds the music still playing
   inside one bubble.

## 8. "The chip knows which sound is your music, so it leaves your music alone."

1. [Literal] Inside the chip: two incoming traces, the song and the hum. A second, inner
   microphone near the ear hears what's left; the chip subtracts the song it knows it sent,
   and only corrects the remainder. Show the song trace passing through untouched, glowing.
2. [Personified] The chip is a bouncer again (callback to line 1), but this time with a guest
   list: music-note guests flash their wristbands and walk past; the hum gets the flip
   treatment.
3. [Analogy] A librarian shushing a room: she shushes the humming air conditioner but leaves
   the person reading aloud the story she asked them to read.
4. [Analogy] Noise-cancelling is a vacuum cleaner that sucks up dust but has a little sensor
   that swerves around your Lego. The Lego pieces are music notes.
5. [Wacky] The chip wears glasses and holds a photo of your playlist; it checks every sound
   against the photo like a detective, waving through anything that matches.
6. [Kid drawing] A crayon sieve: music notes are big so they stay in the bowl, hum
   squiggles are small and fall through and get erased. Grows into: a bigger, sharper sound
   that doesn't fit the sieve neatly.

## 9. "It works best on steady hums, like a plane engine. Sudden sounds, like voices, are harder."

1. [Literal] A plane engine's hum as a smooth, repeating sine wave; the copy locks onto it
   perfectly and the sum is flat. Then a voice arrives as a spiky, irregular shape; the copy
   lags behind it, mismatched, and spikes poke through the flat line.
2. [Analogy] Skipping rope: a steady hum is a rope turning at a steady rhythm, easy to jump
   in time with. A voice is someone jerking the rope at random; the jumper (the chip) trips.
3. [Analogy] Dance partners: the engine waltzes in a perfect predictable loop and the copy
   mirrors every step. Then someone starts breakdancing; the copy flails, a beat late.
4. [Personified] The chef from line 5: the plane hum is a smooth conveyor of identical
   pancakes, flipped effortlessly. Then a pile of random-shaped things (a fork, a sock, a
   "HEY!") comes down the belt and the chef panics, flipping too late.
5. [Wacky] Whack-a-mole: steady hum moles pop up on a clockwork schedule and the chip bonks
   every one. Voice moles pop up at random; the chip's hammer misses again and again.
6. [Change of scale] From inside the cabin out to the whole plane flying: the engine is a
   huge, slow, steady drumbeat; zoom back to the seat where a toddler suddenly shrieks, a
   tiny sharp spike the system can't catch in time. Grows into: the spike flies at the ear
   and hits something soft.

## 10. "The soft cushions help block those."

1. [Literal] The spiky voice wave hits the foam ear cushion and sinks into it, losing energy
   like a ball thrown into a pillow; only a faint whisper gets through.
2. [Analogy] The cushions are a goalkeeper: the chip handles the steady stuff in midfield,
   the cushion dives and catches the sudden shots.
3. [Analogy] A marshmallow wall: sharp, jumpy sounds plunge into it and get stuck like
   thrown darts in a marshmallow.
4. [Wacky] The cushions are two sleeping fluffy sheep wrapped around the ears; voice spikes
   bounce into the wool and get tangled, the sheep don't even wake up.
5. [Personified] Callback: the bouncer from line 1 returns. Turns out his job is real, just
   for the sudden noises; he catches the "HEY!" in a big soft glove and tucks it away. The
   chip and the bouncer fist-bump: teamwork.
6. [Change of scale] Zoom into the foam until its pores are a cave system; the voice wave
   gets lost in the tunnels, bouncing around and fading. Grows into: zoom back out of the
   foam to the whole headphone.

## 11. "So your headphones listen, flip the noise, and fight sound with sound."

1. [Literal] A three-beat recap on one continuous path: the wave enters the mic (listen), passes
   through the chip and turns upside down (flip), leaves the speaker and meets the noise to go
   flat (fight). The flat line becomes the headband arc of the headphones.
2. [Analogy] A judo throw: the noise charges in, the headphones use its own force against it,
   flipping it over and laying it flat on the mat.
3. [Personified] The cast assembles for a curtain call: the spy microphone, the pancake-
   flipper chip, the upside-down backup singer, and the cushion bouncer, bowing together on
   top of the headband while the hum lies flat as a doormat in front of them.
4. [Wacky] Two identical noise monsters, one upside down, punch each other at exactly the same
   time and both pop like soap bubbles. The headphone wearer sips tea in total silence.
5. [Kid drawing] The kid's crayon drawing from line 1 (brick wall in the cup) gets scribbled
   over and redrawn: ear, wavy line, upside-down wavy line, flat line, smiley face. Full
   circle from "block sound" to "fight sound with sound".
6. [Change of scale] Pull back from the flat line at the ear to the passenger, the cabin, the
   plane, the sky: the engine still roars out there, but the frame ends on one small, calm
   bubble of quiet around a pair of headphones.

---

## Through-lines worth considering (to keep one point of focus growing frame to frame)

- **Follow one sound.** A single hum squiggle (with a face, optional) is the camera's guide for
  the whole film: bounced by the bouncer (1), twinned and flipped (2), revealed as air (3),
  overheard by the mic (4), flipped by the chip (5), launched by the speaker (6), erased at the
  ear (7). The voice spike takes over as the guide for 9-10.
- **The bouncer arc.** Starts as the wrong idea (1), returns as the right job for sudden sounds
  (10), so the misconception gets paid off rather than just dismissed.
- **The flat line.** Every cancellation moment ends on a flat line that then becomes the next
  shape (the eardrum at rest, the plane's horizon, the headband arc), so frames grow out of
  each other instead of cutting.
- **Avoid the "moon and arrows" trap.** Never hold a still diagram while labels fade in and out;
  something physical is always moving and changing (dots squeezing, a chef flipping, waves
  colliding), and the camera is always travelling along the sound's path.
