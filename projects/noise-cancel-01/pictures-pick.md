# Noise cancel 01: picture picks

Source: `pictures-brainstorm.md` (written against an earlier draft) judged against the locked
`lines.txt`, which wins where they differ. Differences that matter: line 1 is "Lots of people"
(not "Most"); line 8 now says *why* the chip leaves the music alone ("because it's the one playing
it. So it only flips the noise."), which none of the brainstorm's line-8 ideas show; line 9 says
"Quick, changing sounds" (not "Sudden"), so the voice wave has to keep changing shape. A one-off
spike is not enough.

Format: 1080x1920, 9:16, ~50 s, 11 lines. Timings below are estimates from word count
(about 2.75 words/s); the VO alignment sets the real ones.

---

## 1. The film in one paragraph

One passenger, one window seat, one plane. The hum is the **engine** outside the window from the
first frame, so line 9's "like a plane engine" pays off something we've watched the whole time.
The camera follows **one orange noise squiggle** (a wavy line with two grumpy eyes, printed NOISE)
from the engine into the ear cup. It dives into the squiggle to find it is made of air balls
(line 3), rides it to the microphones, through the chip (a chip with a spatula arm that flips
wave slices) and out of the speaker, where the squiggle meets its upside-down cobalt twin (COPY)
at the eardrum and both pop. Then the camera follows a music note back to where it came from (the
chip's own music box), pulls out to the engine and a chatty neighbour whose VOICE squiggles keep
changing shape, lets the cushion swallow them, and ends on the whole plane in the sky: hum
everywhere, one small calm bubble at one window. Three worlds, each reached through a zoom on one
point, never a cut: the **cabin** (lines 1, 7b, 9–11), the **air** inside a squiggle (line 3, a
short return in 7), and the **machine room** inside the ear cup (lines 2, 4–6, 8).

### Why these worlds earn their place
- **Thought bubble (line 1).** "People think" needs a picture of a thought. The bubble *popping* is
  the picture for "But actually", and the camera falls through the hole into the real ear cup.
- **Air world (line 3).** The mechanism is air, so the film has to show air once at a scale where
  balls visibly crowd and spread. We get there by zooming into the squiggle we're already
  following, and leave by zooming back out until the crowding draws that same squiggle. The world
  change is the explanation ("a sound wave is this").
- **Machine room (lines 4–8).** Mic, chip and speaker are the three parts the voice names, all
  inside one cup. A cutaway cup with a little factory line inside keeps them in one frame the
  camera can travel along.

### Shared vocabulary (built once, reused everywhere: this keeps the build within 90 min)
- **Noise squiggle**: orange wavy path, two eyes, printed NOISE the first time. Steady hum = smooth
  and repeating.
- **Copy squiggle**: the same path flipped vertically, cobalt, eyes upside down, printed COPY.
- **Pop**: when a noise and its copy overlap, both flatten into a straight line, then burst into 8–12
  puff particles. Every cancellation in the film uses this pop, so by line 9 a muted viewer reads
  "pop = cancelled", "no pop = got through".
- **Air balls**: small round balls, a few with dot eyes, printed AIR on one. Crowded = push,
  spread = pull. PUSH and PULL ride on the crowded and spread bands.
- **Music notes**: warm yellow/green notes that bounce, printed MUSIC. Never orange, never flipped.
- Colour only for state: orange = noise, cobalt = copy, yellow/green = music, everything else
  neutral (cabin greys and navy).

### Line 8. "The chip knows your music, because it's the one playing it. So it only flips the noise."
The locked line gives a *reason* the brainstorm never drew. 8.1 (subtract the known song) is the
accurate one but reads as a diagram. 8.2 (bouncer with guest list), 8.3 (librarian), 8.4 (Lego
vacuum), 8.5 (detective with playlist photo) and 8.6 (sieve) all show *sorting*, not *knowing
because I'm playing it*. The sieve is also wrong (size isn't what tells them apart).

**Pick: a new combination. Follow the note home + 8.5's matching check.** Inside the calm bubble
a MUSIC note dances, untouched. The camera follows it back through the cup to where it came from:
the chip's music box, still cranking out notes (the image of "it's the one playing it"). On the

turning drum and lights up, and the spatula stops short. The note hops over and carries on,
glowing. Clarity: the viewer has seen the chip make the music, then sees it spare exactly those
notes. Runner-up: 8.5 alone (detective with a playlist photo). It's cheaper, but it loses
"because it's playing it".

(the carrier into the next line). "World" coordinates: one big SVG scene that the camera

## 5. Build notes (one builder, ~90 min, 4-core CPU, software WebGL)
- **All 2D SVG, no three.js.** Software WebGL buys nothing here, and the zooms are vector.
- **One world, one camera.** Lay the cabin, the cutaway cup machine room and the ball strips out
  in one SVG coordinate space, and drive a single `<g transform>` camera from keyframes per line
  (scale plus translate, eased). That gives the no-cut thread for free. The L3 air world is a
  canvas (or SVG) layer that fades in as camera scale passes ~10x and out again on the way back.
- **Components, built once:** `Squiggle` (path, eyes, colour, `flip` 0→1 rotation, `pop` progress →
  flatten plus puff particles), `AirBalls` (density from `sin(kx − ωt)` plus per-ball jitter seed),
  `Chip` (legs tap, spatula angle), `MusicBox` (drum rotation, note spawner), `Cone` (pump
  offset), `MicStalk` (rise, swivel), `Cabin` (seats, window, engine fan, 4–5 passengers with
  idle loops: nod, sip, hair tremble driven by one `shudder` value), `Label`.
- **Time budget:** world plus camera 15 min · squiggle/pop 15 · air balls 15 · chip/belt/music box/
  cone 20 · cabin plus passengers 15 · plane end frame 5 · timing pass 5.
- **Cut list if behind (in order):** L11's plane pull-back (end on the calm head instead) · L4's
  trolley and vent sources (all three mics face the engine) · L9's chip inset · L1's nodding row
  shrinks to 3 heads. Never cut: the backflip (L2), the ball zoom (L3), the pop at the drum (L7),
  the music box (L6/L8).
- **Check before render:** stills at each line's midpoint, with sound off. Can someone tell where
  the noise is pushing and the copy pulling? Count movers per beat (at least 3 independent ones).
  Make sure no real brand shapes are on the headphones or the plane (no logos, no airline livery).
