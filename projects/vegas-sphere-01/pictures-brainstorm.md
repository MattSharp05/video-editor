# Vegas Sphere 01: picture brainstorm (fresh agent, no production rules)

Picture ideas for vegas-sphere-01 (from /home/user/video-editor/projects/vegas-sphere-01/lines.txt only). No files written.

A through-line that covers all 10 lines: one continuous camera move. It starts on the Sphere at night, zooms down into a single puck, then pulls all the way back out again. The puck (the dot) is the single point of focus that carries every transition. The backgrounds are never black: dusk sky over the Strip, desert orange, a warm interior.

For each line, the ideas are tagged by kind. [HAND-OFF] marks how the frame can grow into the next line.

---

LINE 1: "Most people think the Sphere in Las Vegas is one giant TV."

1. Literal: The Sphere at dusk on the Strip, showing a huge emoji face. A TV remote floats into frame, presses "channel up", and the Sphere obediently flips channels (emoji, then a basketball, then the moon). The viewer is in on the myth.
2. Everyday analogy: A boxy old CRT TV sits in the desert. Someone inflates it like a beach ball until it rounds into the Sphere, antenna still poking out the top. A rabbit-ears antenna on the Sphere is a good running gag for "the myth".
3. Wacky: A giant hand peels a shrink-wrap screen protector off the Sphere, the way you would off a new phone. This shows the "one big sheet of glass" belief.
4. Personified: The Sphere itself, with an eyeball face, says "I'm a TV" proudly in a speech bubble. A tourist below nods along.
5. Kid's drawing: A crayon TV with a big round screen and a plug cord running to a wall socket in the desert. The plug is the joke: "where would you even plug it in?"
[HAND-OFF] The camera starts drifting toward one patch of the "screen", as if something seems off about it.

LINE 2: "But actually, it's over a million glowing dots, with gaps between them."

1. Literal: The camera keeps pushing into the TV-myth image, and the smooth picture breaks apart into a grid of glowing dots with dark sky showing through the gaps. It's one continuous zoom, not a cut.
2. Wacky: The record-scratch moment. A finger pokes the "screen" and goes straight through a gap. A pigeon flies through the gap too.
3. Everyday analogy: A Lite-Brite board. Pegs pop in one by one until they form the emoji face from line 1. The gaps are the black board showing.
4. Change of scale: A tiny tourist rappels down the "screen" and finds it is a climbing wall. Each handhold is a glowing dot and there is open air between them.
5. Kid's drawing: A kid's dot-to-dot picture of the Sphere where the dots never got connected and stay as dots.
[HAND-OFF] Stop the zoom on one dot, which grows to fill a third of the frame.

LINE 3: "The outside is covered in lights shaped like hockey pucks. There are about one point two million."

1. Literal: The dot we stopped on turns out to be a puck shape. Pucks ripple outward across the curve of the Sphere like a wave filling a stadium.
2. Wacky: A hockey player slapshots a puck at the Sphere and it sticks with a "thunk". Then a hailstorm of 1.2 million pucks sticks all over it like Velcro, and a counter spins up to 1,200,000.
3. Everyday analogy: A disco ball, but each mirror tile swapped for a glowing puck. Or a golf ball's dimples, each dimple lighting up.
4. Change of scale: Stack the 1.2 million pucks into a tower next to the Sphere, or spread them out over football fields, to make the number felt rather than read.
5. Personified: The pucks are a crowd of tiny round citizens filing into their seats on the Sphere, like a stadium filling up before a show.
[HAND-OFF] Peel one puck back from the surface to reveal what it's mounted on.

LINE 4: "They sit on a steel frame, about eight inches apart. Each puck holds forty eight tiny lights."

1. Literal: The camera tilts behind the pucks to show the steel lattice. A tape measure snaps out between two pucks and reads 8". Then the camera dives into one puck face and the 48 tiny diodes light up, counted off in a quick sweep.
2. Everyday analogy: An egg carton. The steel frame is the carton and each puck is an egg in its slot with a gap between. Inside one egg (puck) are 48 seeds, like a pomegranate cut open.
3. Change of scale: A human hand next to a puck for size. A ruler shows 8 inches, about a sub sandwich, and an actual sandwich slides into the gap between two pucks.
4. Wacky: Each puck is a tiny muffin tin with 48 glowing cupcakes in it.
5. Kid's drawing: The steel frame as a jungle gym with pucks hanging like lanterns, and 48 little star stickers drawn on each one.
[HAND-OFF] The 48 lights blend together and the puck glows a single colour, filling the frame.

LINE 5: "Each puck is one dot of the picture. It can glow in millions of colors."

1. Literal: The single puck cycles through colours fast, like a mood ring spun at high speed.
2. Everyday analogy: One square of a cross-stitch, or one tile in a mosaic. Pull back slightly and the puck is a single tile in a small mosaic of a face.
3. Wacky: A paint-chip wall at a hardware store that never ends. The puck flips through swatches like a slot machine and stops on "Vegas Sunset Orange".
4. Personified: The puck is a chameleon, or an actor changing costumes at lightning speed in a dressing room.
5. Kid's drawing: A giant box of crayons, millions of them, and the puck dips into one after another.
[HAND-OFF] A thin wire runs from the puck off-frame, and the camera follows it.

LINE 6: "A computer tells every puck what color to show, many times a second."

1. Literal: Follow the wire to a computer in a control room. Glowing messages ("orange", "blue", "off") shoot out along the wires to the pucks like pneumatic-tube mail, over and over.
2. Everyday analogy: A stadium card stunt. A coach with a megaphone yells colours and the crowd flips cards. Here the crowd is the pucks, and the shouting happens 60 times a second.
3. Personified: The computer is an orchestra conductor and the pucks are a choir of a million singers, each holding up a coloured note. The baton moves at a blur.
4. Wacky: A tiny mail carrier sprinting puck to puck delivering colour envelopes, cloned a million times, all running at once.
5. Change of scale: A flipbook. Each page is one instruction set, and the thumb riffles it to show "many times a second".
[HAND-OFF] The conductor's baton points outward and the camera starts pulling back from the pucks.

LINE 7: "Up close, you see dots and dark gaps. From far away, your eyes blend them into one picture."

This line pays off line 2, so make it the most satisfying move in the video: one long continuous pull-back.
1. Literal: Pull back from the pucks. The dots and gaps resolve into a picture, and the moment of blending happens on screen.
2. Everyday analogy: A pointillist painting (Seurat style). The view starts nose-to-canvas on dots of paint, then steps back to see a park scene. Or a newspaper photo under a magnifying glass.
3. Personified / physical: A viewer's eyeball walks backward across the parking lot. The farther it goes, the more its "blur" slider rises and the dots melt into an image.
4. Change of scale: Split the frame with a person squinting. Squint and the dots merge, open the eyes and the dots return. Invite the viewer to try it on their own phone screen, which is also dots.
5. Wacky: Dots drift together like magnets as the camera backs away, then spring apart when it moves closer again, as if the dots are shy.
[HAND-OFF] The picture that forms is a giant eye on the Sphere.

LINE 8: "That's how a giant eyeball can blink on the whole building."

1. Literal: The famous eyeball blinks. Mid-blink, zoom quickly into the eyelid and show that it's just rows of pucks switching from white to skin colour in sequence. Then snap back out.
2. Everyday analogy: A stadium wave. The eyelid comes down as a wave of pucks "standing up" in a new colour, row by row.
3. Personified: The eyeball notices the camera, blinks, then winks at the viewer. It looks around and follows a car driving by on the Strip.
4. Wacky: The eyeball gets something in it. A giant hand with a tissue reaches in, the eye blinks, and a single puck "tear" rolls down the Sphere.
5. Kid's drawing: A flipbook of the eye in crayon. Each page is a puck grid, and riffling it makes the blink.
[HAND-OFF] The eye opens wide, and the pupil becomes a doorway the camera flies through into the inside.

LINE 9: "Inside, there's a second screen. It's much sharper, and it wraps over your head."

1. Literal: Fly through the pupil into the interior bowl. The audience is in seats and the screen curves overhead like the sky. A dot-size comparison shows the outer pucks as fat marbles next to fine grains of sand inside.
2. Everyday analogy: A planetarium dome, or lying under a giant umbrella with a movie printed on the inside.
3. Change of scale: The Sphere as a hollow egg. The outside is the shell with spots and the inside is a smooth, sharp lining. Crack the egg open to see both.
4. Wacky: Someone puts on a giant VR headset, and the headset turns out to be the whole Sphere, with the audience sitting inside the lens.
5. Comparison with motion: A magnifying glass slides from an outside puck (big dots, big gaps) to the inside screen (dots so tight you can't see gaps). It shows the "sharper" claim instead of stating it.
[HAND-OFF] Pull back out through the roof, out of the Sphere, and keep rising.

LINE 10: "So the Sphere is a million little lights, and your eyes do the rest."

1. Literal: Keep rising until the whole Sphere sits glowing on the Strip, then the whole city. Las Vegas itself becomes a field of dots, the same idea at a bigger scale.
2. Callback: The TV remote from line 1 floats back in, reaches for the Sphere, and gets shooed off by the eyeball. The myth is busted and the loop is closed.
3. Personified: The viewer's two eyes appear as the "co-workers" who do the rest. A little pair of eyeballs takes a bow next to the Sphere.
4. Wacky / loop: Zoom into a single light in the Vegas skyline and it is a puck again. This makes a seamless loop back to line 2 for rewatches.
5. Kid's drawing: A crayon drawing of a million dots on a ball, with the caption "your eyes did this", and a fridge magnet pinning it up.
