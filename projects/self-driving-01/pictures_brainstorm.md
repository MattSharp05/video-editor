# Pictures brainstorm: self-driving-01

Format: ~60 s vertical, one continuous shot (no cuts). Each new frame grows out of the last with one point of focus.
Look: 3D renders, realistic assets, engaging motion.
Each line has ideas of different kinds: literal, analogy, wacky/impossible, scale change, personified, kid's drawing.
Each idea says what we see and what happens to it. A **Grows from / into** note says how it could join the one-shot chain.

---

## 1. "There's nobody driving this car. So how does it see you?"

**1a. Literal: the empty seat, push-in.** We look through a rainy windscreen at a white robotaxi parked at a crosswalk. The driver's seat is empty and the steering wheel turns slowly by itself. The camera pushes through the glass to the empty seat, then spins 180° to look out the windscreen at *you*, a pedestrian stepping off the kerb. A faint green outline traces around your body.
*Grows into:* the outline on you becomes the camera's focus for line 2.

**1b. Wacky: the car blinks.** Front-on hero shot of the robotaxi at night. Its roof sensor dome works like an eyeball. It swivels, the iris (the spinning lidar) narrows, and it *blinks*: a shutter of light sweeps across the street. Everything it passed over turns into glowing dots for one frame, then back.
*Grows into:* the dome's iris becomes the "0" in "2004".

**1c. Personified: the steering wheel with nobody's hands.** Extreme close-up of a steering wheel turning on its own, with ghostly outlines of hands that *aren't there* fading in and out. Then the wheel stops and the camera rises to the windscreen: a crowd of pedestrians, each one tagged one by one with a little box.

**1d. Point of view: you are the target.** The shot starts as the car's view: a grey point-cloud world, and a single figure (the viewer) lit in hot orange in the middle of the frame with a tag: `PEDESTRIAN 0.98`. The camera pulls back out *through* the lidar dome and we see the real, photographic car looking at the real person.
*Why it might win:* the hook question ("how does it see you?") is answered visually in the first second: like *this*. Then the video explains it.

**1e. Kid's drawing: the car with eyes.** A crayon car with two big cartoon eyes on the windscreen and nobody inside. The crayon drawing "inflates" into a photoreal 3D robotaxi, and the cartoon eyes shrink into the real sensors on the roof and bumpers.

## 2. "In 2004, not one robot car could finish a desert race. This year, Waymo passed half a million rides a week, with nobody at the wheel."

**2a. Literal: desert to city, one road.** Pull out from the car's sensor to a dusty 2004 Mojave track. A boxy, sensor-laden pickup truck lurches, stalls and drifts into a berm, steaming. A distance counter freezes at "7.4 mi / 142". Camera flies forward down the same road; the sand turns to asphalt, the asphalt to a city street, and the dead truck is overtaken by a stream of white robotaxis.

**2b. Scale change: one car becomes half a million.** A single wrecked desert robot car on a dusty plateau, seen from above. The camera rises fast; every map tile we pass fills with tiny white cars, until the whole frame is a glittering river of 500,000 dots flowing through a city grid like blood through veins.
*Why it might win:* "half a million a week" is unfeelable as a number. As a swarm, it hits.

**2c. Wacky: the trophy nobody collected.** A gold trophy sits alone at a desert finish line in 2004, with tumbleweed rolling past and a "finish" banner flapping. Time-lapse: the banner rots, and the desert becomes city around the trophy. When a robotaxi finally rolls through the finish line, the trophy is surrounded by traffic.

**2d. Analogy: a toddler's first steps to a sprinter.** A toddling robot car wobbles, falls over in the sand (like a baby), and, in one continuous shot, its "footprints" in the sand turn into tyre tracks that become a stream of cars hurrying past.

**2e. Literal counter: the ride ticker.** A mechanical flip-counter (like an old airport departure board) on the side of a robotaxi: `0` in 2004 flips furiously up to `500,000 / week`. Each flip throws off a small white car that drives away out of frame.

---

## 3. "First, the car has to see you. On its roof, lidar fires laser pulses and times each bounce, building a 3D picture of the street."

**3a. Literal: the point cloud draws the street.** Camera dives onto the roof of one taxi, close to the spinning lidar. It fires thin red beams; each beam hits a surface (a lamp post, a cyclist, your jacket) and leaves one glowing dot where it hit. Thousands of beams later, the street exists only as dots, and you are a person made of dots.

**3b. Analogy: a bat, or throwing tennis balls.** A tiny figure on the roof throws tennis balls at everything and listens for when they bounce back: near things return fast (*tok*), far things slow (*... tok*). Each return leaves a pin stuck where the ball hit. Speed up a million times: the pins form the street.
*Why it might win:* "times each bounce" becomes obvious: near = fast, far = slow.

**3c. Wacky: the car paints the city with a laser paint gun.** The lidar is a spinning paint sprayer. Every pulse splats a glowing dot of paint onto the world, until the real street is fully coated in neon and the photographic layer peels away, leaving only the painted 3D sculpture of the street.

**3d. Scale change: ride along on one photon.** The camera *becomes* one laser pulse. We shoot out of the lidar, fly across the street at absurd speed, smack into your shoulder, and bounce back. A stopwatch in the corner reads `0.000 000 067 s`, and a dot appears on your shoulder. Then pull back to see millions of others doing the same.

**3e. Kid's drawing: connect the dots.** The street is a blank page; the lidar draws dots one by one, then a crayon draws lines connecting them and a car, a tree and *you* appear, like a child's dot-to-dot puzzle, but in 3D, rotating.

**3f. Personified: the lidar as a blind man with a cane.** A spinning lidar "taps" the world with a long thin beam like a white cane, and each tap leaves a mark. Very fast tapping turns the marks into a full picture.

## 4. "Radar tracks speed, even in rain. Cameras read traffic lights and signs."

**4a. Literal: rain on, layers on.** It starts raining on the dot-street. Lidar dots get noisy, speckled by raindrops. Radar ripples (wide, soft rings) roll out from the bumper and pass straight through the rain; a cyclist gets a speed tag `14 mph →`. Then the camera lens on the windscreen glints, and the traffic light turns from grey dots into a real image with `RED`, and the stop sign reads `STOP`.

**4b. Analogy: three senses, three superheroes.** The car's three sensors turn into three characters on its roof: Lidar (a sculptor shaping the street), Radar (a figure in a raincoat, unfazed, holding a speed gun at cars) and Camera (a reader with glasses, reading signs aloud). Each does its one job in turn, then they merge back into the sensor pod.

**4c. Wacky: rain stops in mid-air.** Time freezes. Raindrops hang in the air. A radar wave rolls through them untouched, hits a car, and comes back squashed (the Doppler effect drawn as the wave crumpling like a concertina) and reads its speed. Then time restarts.

**4d. Change of scale: an X-ray of the bumper.** The camera slides into the front bumper. Behind the plastic sits a small radar chip; we watch its waves leave through the bumper skin and come back stretched or squeezed, telling apart approaching and leaving cars.

**4e. Kid's drawing: eyes, ears and nose.** The car gets three crayon "faces": lidar = hands feeling shapes, radar = ears listening through rain, camera = eyes reading a sign. The child's labels morph into the real sensors.

---

## 5. "But seeing isn't enough. It predicts where every person, bike and car might go next."

**5a. Literal: ghost futures.** Everything freezes. From every person, bike and car, translucent "ghost" copies fan out ahead of them along possible paths, brightest for the likeliest. The pedestrian (you) gets three ghosts: one crossing, one stopping, one turning back.
*Why it might win:* prediction made visible as ghosts is instantly readable and beautiful in 3D.

**5b. Analogy: a goalkeeper.** The pedestrian becomes a penalty taker; the car becomes a goalkeeper reading the hips and feet, predicting where the ball (the person) will go before they move. Back on the street, the same posture reading: a shoulder turns, so the "crossing" ghost brightens.

**5c. Wacky: everyone's thought bubbles.** Every road user gets a cartoon thought bubble with their plan: a dog wants the pigeon, a cyclist wants to turn left, a kid wants the ice cream van across the road. The car reads all bubbles at once, and the risky one (the kid) glows red.

**5d. Scale change: the chess board.** The camera rises straight up until the intersection becomes a chessboard: people, bikes and cars become pieces, and each shows its legal next moves as glowing squares. The car is a piece calculating everyone's moves at once.

**5e. Personified: the car squints.** The headlights narrow like eyes as a ball bounces into the street from between parked cars. Before any child appears, a ghost child is predicted running after it.

---

## 6. "Then it plans a path around all of them, again and again as they move."

**6a. Literal: a green ribbon re-routes.** A glowing green path ribbon lays itself out in front of the car, weaving through the predicted ghosts. Every time someone moves, the ribbon snaps to a new shape: 10 times a second, shown as a rapid flicker of ribbons.

**6b. Analogy: walking through a crowd at a train station.** The car becomes a person weaving through a busy station crowd, constantly adjusting each step around others. Then the person becomes the car again doing the same thing on the street.

**6c. Wacky: thousands of tiny cars try every option.** From the front bumper, hundreds of tiny ghost cars burst out down every possible route. Most crash into a ghost and pop like soap bubbles; one survives, glows green, and the real car follows it. It happens again, and again, every fraction of a second.
*Why it might win:* shows "planning" as trial and elimination, funny and clear.

**6d. Scale change: the 0.1 second loop.** A clock face around the car splits each second into 10 slices; each slice re-draws the plan. Slowed down, we see the ribbon change in each slice as a cyclist drifts left.

**6e. Kid's drawing: a maze.** The street turns into a printed maze puzzle in a kids' activity book; a pencil finds the path, but then the maze walls (people) slide, the pencil erases and redraws, over and over.

## 7. "It practised over 20 billion miles in simulation."

**7a. Literal: the simulator.** The camera pulls back from the street and we see it was a hologram on a table, one of millions of identical glowing tiles of street scenes, stretching to the horizon, each with a car practising something different: snow, a deer, a mattress falling off a truck.
*Grows from/into:* the "real" street turns out to be one tile, then we zoom back into one.

**7b. Scale change: miles to the Sun.** A line of road unrolls from the car's wheel, off the street, up into the sky, past the Moon, past Mars, out to the Sun and back... a label shows 20 billion miles = 100+ round trips to the Sun. Then the road snaps back like a tape measure.

**7c. Wacky: a car's dream.** The robotaxi parked at night with its lights dimmed is "sleeping"; a dream bubble rises above it with a full simulated city, where the dream car dodges chaos: a giraffe, a parade, a sofa in the road. A text pops: `20,000,000,000 mi`.
*Why it might win:* memorable, funny, accurate in spirit (practise without danger), and great for 3D.

**7d. Analogy: a pilot's flight simulator.** A cockpit simulator box on hydraulic legs; inside, not a pilot, but a robotaxi. It bounces through storms and near misses in a flight-simulator capsule.

**7e. Personified: the training montage.** A Rocky-style montage: the car does "reps" against obstacles in a simulated gym, sweat (coolant) dripping, rep counter climbing to 20 billion.

## 8. "Tesla bet differently: cameras only, no lidar, and one neural network that turns video into steering."

**8a. Literal: two cars side by side.** The camera pans from the Waymo (sensor dome on top) to a sleek Tesla with no roof sensors. The lidar dome on an imaginary Tesla pops off and falls away. Eight small camera "eyes" around the Tesla light up, and video tiles from each flow into one glowing brain-like network, which outputs a single steering wheel turning.

**8b. Analogy: driving the way you do, with only your eyes.** A person driving with just their eyes. Their eyes become the Tesla's cameras: "you don't have lasers, you have eyes and a brain." Video goes into the eyes, and hands turn the wheel.

**8c. Wacky: the video funnel.** Video footage pours like liquid from eight cameras into a funnel (the neural network). Out of the bottom comes one thin stream that turns the steering wheel like a water wheel.
*Why it might win:* "one network turns video into steering" becomes a one-glance machine: video in, steering out, nothing in between.

**8d. Scale change: inside the network.** Fly into the Tesla's computer; a frame of video breaks into a million glowing pixels, which pass through layers of connected neurons that light up in waves, finally collapsing into one arrow: `steer 4° left`.

**8e. Personified: the poker table.** Two cars at a poker table: Waymo pushes in chips stamped "lidar, radar, maps"; Tesla pushes all its chips on one card labelled "cameras". "Bet differently" literally.

## 9. "Most Teslas still need a driver watching. But some Tesla robotaxis in Texas now run with no employee on board."

**9a. Literal: hands hovering, then gone.** Inside a Tesla, a driver's hands hover over the wheel, eyes on the road (an eye-tracking camera watches the driver). Camera moves along the street, across a "Texas" road sign, to another Tesla: its seats are empty, both front and back.

**9b. Analogy: training wheels.** A bike with training wheels (driver watching) rolls along. The camera follows it into Austin, where one bike's training wheels pop off and it rides on. The bike turns into a Tesla robotaxi.

**9c. Wacky: the driver evaporates.** A safety driver sits in a Tesla. As the car crosses the Texas state line, he fades away like a ghost, giving a little wave, and the car drives on with no one.

**9d. Scale change: a map of Texas lights up.** Zoom up from the car to the map of the US; most Tesla dots glow with a tiny "person icon" in them, but a small cluster in Texas shows empty cars. Zoom down into one.

**9e. Kid's drawing: a car with a babysitter.** Most cars drawn with a grown-up beside the child at the wheel; one car in Texas drawn alone, cowboy hat on its roof, driving off.

---

## 10. "So, is it cracked? Not everywhere. But in about 15 US cities, a car with nobody inside already sees you, guesses your next step, and drives around you."

**10a. Literal: the map of 15 cities, then back to you.** The camera rises to a night map of the US; about 15 cities light up as points, the rest stay dark (not everywhere). Then dive back down into one city, one street, the very first crosswalk: the car sees you (dots), guesses your next step (ghost), and drives around you (green ribbon). Full loop to shot 1.
*Why it might win:* one shot ends where it began; the three verbs recap all three mechanisms in one beat.

**10b. Wacky: a cracked egg.** A giant egg sits on the map of America with cracks on it, light bleeding out of about 15 of the cracks. "Is it cracked?" Literally: partly. A robotaxi peeks out of one crack like a hatching chick.

**10c. Analogy: a jigsaw puzzle.** The US map as a jigsaw; 15 pieces are placed and glowing, the rest are still in the box. A final piece hovers.

**10d. Personified: the car waves.** Back at the crosswalk, the robotaxi stops for you, its sensor dome "nods", and you cross. As you walk away, the camera stays on the empty driver's seat. End frame mirrors the opening.

**10e. Scale change: from orbit to your shoelace.** Start from a satellite view with 15 glowing cities, then one fast continuous zoom from orbit down to a single city, a single street, a single crosswalk, and the one lidar dot landing on *your shoelace*.

---

## Strongest five (picker's suggestion)

1. **1d: You are the target** - opens in the car's point-cloud vision with the viewer tagged, and pulls out through the lidar dome into the real car. Answers the hook in the first second and sets the visual language.
2. **3b: Tennis balls / near-fast far-slow** - makes "times each bounce" understandable without any words; it sticks.
3. **5a: Ghost futures** - prediction drawn as translucent copies fanning out; instantly readable, gorgeous in 3D, and the ghost can carry straight into line 6.
4. **6c: Tiny ghost cars try every route and pop** - planning shown as trial and elimination, re-run every fraction of a second; funny and accurate.
5. **10a: Loop back to the first crosswalk** - the three closing verbs (sees, guesses, drives around) are replayed as dots, ghost, ribbon on the same street as shot 1, closing the one-shot loop.

Honourable mentions: **2b** (one wreck to a river of 500,000 cars), **7c** (the car dreams its 20 billion miles), **8c** (video poured through a funnel into a steering wheel).
