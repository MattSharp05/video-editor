## Draft caption
One straight cut through a cube can make a perfect hexagon. Aim the knife square-on to the line from one corner to
the opposite corner, through the centre. Did you guess square? Try it on cheese and tell me what you got.

## Claims to verify
1. A flat cut through a cube can have at most six sides, one per face crossed (the cube is convex, so the cut meets
   each face in at most one segment). Checked: true; the possible sections are triangle, quadrilateral (square,
   rectangle, trapezoid…), pentagon and hexagon. Source: MathWorld "Cube" (plane sections); Wikipedia "Cube" /
   "Hexagon" (regular hexagon as the cross-section perpendicular to a space diagonal).
2. The cut through the centre, perpendicular to the corner-to-corner (space) diagonal, passes through the midpoints of
   six edges and is a regular hexagon. Checked by computation in this session: plane x+y+z=1.5 meets the unit cube at
   (½,0,1), (0,½,1), (0,1,½), (½,1,0), (1,½,0), (1,0,½); every side is √½ of the cube's edge.
3. Line l1 is framed as a belief ("most people think"); a straight-down cut does give a square, as shown.
