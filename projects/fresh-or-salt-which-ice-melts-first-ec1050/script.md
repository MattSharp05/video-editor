# Fresh or salt: which ice melts first: script v1 (2026-11-27)

**Idea:** in a glass of room-temperature water, an ice cube melts faster in fresh water than in salt water, because
fresh meltwater sinks and stirs the glass, while in salt water it floats as a cold layer around the cube.
**Goal:** reach. Genre: brainteaser. Voice r3-m07, drafted at 2.7 words/s. Hook from the routine planner (question

Colour language: blue = cold meltwater (dyed, the lead in the explanation); warm amber = warm water moving; the
ice cubes are clear white; FRESH and SALT tags on the glasses keep one place each (fresh left, salt right).

## Alternative hooks
- "Salt melts ice on roads. In a glass, it loses." | SALT LOSES THIS RACE (flag: nothing checkable)
- "This ice cube melts twice as slow, in salt water." | SAME ICE. SLOWER MELT. (flag: 3.7 s; "twice" unverified)

## Claims to verify
- Ice melts faster in fresh room-temperature water than in salt water (density-driven convection vs a stable fresh
  layer).
- Cold (near 0 °C) fresh meltwater is denser than room-temperature fresh water, so it sinks.
- Fresh meltwater is less dense than salt water, so it floats and pools around the cube.
- Road salt melts ice by lowering its freezing point (used only as the reason people guess salt).
