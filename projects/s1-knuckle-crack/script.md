# s1 Knuckle crack: script (v0.1, picture-first)

**Idea:** the pop of a cracked knuckle is a gas bubble forming in the joint fluid, not bones grinding, and cracking
your knuckles hasn't been shown to cause arthritis. **Goal:** reach. **Format:** Instagram Reel 1080x1920, 30 fps,
~36 s. **Voice:** m5. **Music:** Floating Cities. Every picture event is cued from a word (`film/src/timeline.ts`).

## Lines (after the fact check)
1. Lots of people think cracking your knuckles gives you arthritis.
2. So what's actually popping?
3. Each knuckle sits in a sealed capsule of fluid.
4. Pull the bones apart, and the pressure in that fluid drops.
5. A gas bubble forms in the fluid, right at the pop.
6. No bone breaks. Nothing grinds.
7. A knuckle usually can't crack again for about twenty minutes.
8. Scientists think the leftover gas needs time to dissolve.
9. One doctor cracked only his left hand's knuckles, twice a day, for fifty years.
10. Neither hand got arthritis.
11. A study of over two hundred people found no link either.
12. If it ever hurts or swells, stop and get it checked.

## Fact check (fresh agent, 2026-11-19)
Sources: Kawchuk et al. 2015 PLoS ONE 10(4):e0119470 (real-time MRI, one participant: gap 0.93 -> 1.89 mm; "rapid
cavity inception associated with concurrent sound production"; "the resulting cavity was never seen to collapse");
Chandran Suja & Barakat 2018 Sci Rep 8:4600 (partial collapse can explain the sound; ~20 min before a joint cracks
again; gases "take time to be reabsorbed"); Unger 1998 Arthritis Rheum 41(5):949-50 ("For 50 years ... left hand at
least twice a day ... at least 36,500 times ... no arthritis in either hand"); deWeber et al. 2011 J Am Board Fam Med
24(2):169-74 (215 people; cracking "does not seem to be a risk factor for hand OA"); Castellanos & Axelrod 1990 (more
hand swelling, lower grip in crackers); Boutin et al. 2017 Clin Orthop Relat Res (no immediate harm, 40 people);
Arthritis Foundation myths page; Harvard Health blog 2018.
