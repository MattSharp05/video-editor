# Reference reels (studied 2026-11-04)

Both are by Charles Zheng (@charles.zhengg), who wrote cstack. I downloaded them to the session
scratchpad only (not committed: they're his content), then transcribed them locally (Whisper) and
made frame contact sheets. Notes below are from transcripts, frames and measurement. I did not
listen to them.

## A. "How does sand think?" (Dev8BHVBea_)
https://www.instagram.com/reel/Dev8BHVBea_/ · posted 2026-10-21 · 2:21 · ~723k likes, ~5k comments

An animated explainer (animated-video skill) with short on-camera presenter inserts.
- **Hook, frame 0:** a text question ("How does **sand** think?") over a real object, already
  moving. First line: "This is a grain of sand. Melt it, grow it into a crystal, slice it thin,
  and carve it with light. Now, it gained the ability to think."
- **Second sentence raises the stakes, about the viewer:** "In the time it took for you to blink,
  the chip in your phone playing this video made billions of calculations."
- **Every line is an event on screen:** a switch turns on → "1"; two in a row → an AND gate.
  Big numbers are shown as type ("100,000,000,000", "1 + 1 =", "5:51").
- **A chain of "if X, then Y":** add → arithmetic → anything; it keeps escalating.
- **A scale trick near the end:** "slow down time so one tick takes one second" → memory takes 6
  minutes.
- **The presenter appears on camera 4–5 times,** about 3 s each, at turns in the story. It reads
  as a person talking to you, not a slideshow.
- **Look:** dark navy world, one warm accent (orange and gold light) on what the viewer should
  follow, white sans-serif titles. Captions sit low and centred, with the key word on an orange
  box. Very close to Trip's black-and-#FF6B22 system.
- **Ending:** a callback to the opening ("A grain of sand and the magic of human ingenuity…").

## B. "Here's how I created the animation" (DeV22xOBnRC)
https://www.instagram.com/reel/DeV22xOBnRC/ · posted 2026-10-11 · 1:03 · ~6.2k likes · −14.2 LUFS

A behind-the-scenes talking head about the cstack workflow.
- **Proof in the first 2 s:** the earlier film's phone shot, then its analytics ("9,799,110
  views, 54 s average watch time").
- **Screen recordings float as 3D panes** in the same dark world as the film (Claude Code
  terminal, the board of frames, the Resolve timeline), cut between talking-head shots.
- Captions are 1–5 words at a time, with the key word coloured.
- Process as he describes it: script and plan → pick a style → plan key frames with Claude
  (animated-video) → Claude builds the animation while he films VO and talking head → he hands raw
  footage to Claude (video-edit), which assembles music, effects, visuals and cuts.

  sentence.

- Real app screens can live inside the animated world: a phone or floating panes, as in B.
