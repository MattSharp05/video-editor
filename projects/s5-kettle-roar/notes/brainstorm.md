# Picture brainstorm (fresh agent, 2026-11-19; direct.md brief, no production rules) — condensed

Draft lines: 1 "Your kettle is loudest just before it boils. Then it goes quiet." 2 "Here's why." 3 "The water touching the
hot element heats past boiling first, and makes tiny bubbles of steam." 4 "But the water above is still cooler. So each
bubble rises into colder water, and collapses." 5 "Thousands of tiny collapses every second: that's the roar." 6 "As all
the water heats up, the bubbles stop collapsing. They make it to the top and pop gently." 7 "So when it goes quiet, it's
actually boiling." Agent's physics note: the bubbles are steam turning back into water, not air; avoid air-bubble looks.

1. Hook: kettle with a live sound-level meter that climbs into the red then drops as the steam plume appears; stadium
   crowd quiet at the goal; cartoon kettle screams then sips tea; crayon "RRRRR" bubble shrinks to "blub"; person in bed
   suspicious of the silence; an apartment block of kettles going quiet window by window.
2. Here's why: kettle turns X-ray, camera dives through the lid; zipper unzips the kettle; shrink Magic-School-Bus
   style to the glowing element; a dotted cutaway line and the front half falls away.
3. Element boils first: cutaway heat map, a thin red layer hugging the element, blue above, steam bubbles beading on
   the metal; molecules hopping on a hot sidewalk; popcorn on a griddle only; microscopic pits in the metal each
   blowing a bubble (nucleation); crayon red zigzag with circles stuck to it.
4. Rise and collapse: a bubble leaves the red layer into blue water, shrinks and snaps shut; breath on a cold window;
   bubble diver into a freezing lake, "CLAP"; balloons into a cactus ceiling labelled "cold water"; a house-sized bubble
   rising from lava into an icy ocean, crumpling like a crushed can, a shockwave ripple.
5. The roar: split screen, slow-motion collapses with flashes beside a waveform building from spikes into a band; rain
   on a tin roof; bubble wrap under a steamroller; tiny cymbal players; a counter "1 click... 10... 5,000 per second"
   merging into one roar; a drummer kettle.
6. Quiet: the blue turns red from the bottom up, bubbles rise all the way and burp softly, the waveform calms; the
   lake warms up; cactus ceiling melts; pull back to fizz like soda; crayon kettle all red with "blub".
7. Payoff: the kettle clicks off, "100 C", gentle bubbling, the meter low; a toddler loudest before sleep; the opening
   meter again with labelled zones: red "collapsing (not boiling yet)", low "BOILING".

Giant devices: (A) a tall cutaway kettle coloured by temperature with the red "boiling line" climbing; bubbles survive
only below it; each implosion fires a bright ring to the wall, and each ring prints a spike on a waveform strip; (B) a
click counter / meter where each collapse drops a glowing dot, rate = loudness, the stream stops at the end; (C) tiny
cymbal people clapping per collapse. Agent's picks: A + the cold-window analogy for clarity; C + the crushed-can
implosion + the tea-sipping kettle for memory.
