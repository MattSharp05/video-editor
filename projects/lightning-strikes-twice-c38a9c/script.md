## Draft caption
Lightning never strikes twice? Tell that to the Empire State Building. It gets hit about 20 to 25 times a year,
because a tall, pointed tower is the closest point to the cloud. Stay safe: indoors when you hear thunder.

## Claims to verify
- Empire State Building hit about 20-25 times a year (NWS lightning safety myths: "The Empire State Building is hit
  an average of 23 times a year"; https://www.weather.gov/safety/lightning-myths).
- Stepped leader descends from the cloud in steps (~50 m each) and upward streamers rise from tall objects; the
  first to connect completes the channel; the return stroke is the bright flash (NWS "How lightning forms",
  https://www.weather.gov/safety/lightning-science-overview).
- Tall objects are struck more because they are closer to the cloud and concentrate the field.

## Fact check (fresh agent, 2026-11-27)
- Fixed: hook "twenty" -> "over twenty" (NWS: average 23 a year); "holds a huge electric charge" -> "fills up with
  electric charge" (clouds hold separated charges; NSSL); "closest point ... for miles" -> "closest point to the cloud"
  (Midtown has other skyscrapers); "It strikes where" -> "It often strikes where" (tall objects are more likely, not certain).
- Holds: stepped leader in jumps, streamers up from tall things, first to touch makes the path, the bright flash
  (return stroke) along that path, 20-25 a year (NWS weather.gov/safety/lightning-myths; NSSL
  nssl.noaa.gov/education/svrwx101/lightning/; weather.gov/safety/lightning-science-connection-ground).
- Simplification kept: many Empire State Building strikes are upward flashes that start at the building
  (weather.gov/safety/lightning-science-upward-leaders); no line becomes false. Picture: the return stroke brightens from
  the mast upward; the leader stays fainter than the return stroke; neighbouring skyscrapers stay in the wide shot.
