## Claims and sources (fresh fact-check agent, 2026-11-26)
- Sunlight contains all visible colours; the blue sky is Rayleigh scattering by N2/O2: NASA Space Place
  https://spaceplace.nasa.gov/blue-sky/en/ ; NWS https://www.weather.gov/fgz/SkyBlue . Holds.
- "about five times more than red": 1/λ^4. 450 vs 700 nm gives 5.9x; 470 vs 700 nm 4.9x (Stull, Practical
  Meteorology 22.3; Britannica "Rayleigh scattering"). The film's simulation and counter use 470 nm so the on-screen
  numbers (~4.9x) match the spoken "about five" (changed from 450 nm after the check).
- "Red mostly keeps going straight": ~96 % of 700 nm light crosses the air overhead unscattered. Holds.
- "At sunset ... much more air": path ~10-38x longer near the horizon (air mass). Holds.
- "most of the blue gets bounced aside": ~10 % of blue reaches you at a 5 deg sun, ~0 at the horizon
  (https://aty.sdsu.edu/explain/extinction/extintro.html). Holds.
- "Only the reds and oranges are left": slightly simplified (some yellow), fine at this level.
- Orange band along the terminator seen from space: realistic (ISS sunrise photos, NASA Earth Observatory).
- Air drawn ~4x thicker than real: labelled "air drawn thicker than real" in the space shots.
- Violet left out: no line becomes false.
