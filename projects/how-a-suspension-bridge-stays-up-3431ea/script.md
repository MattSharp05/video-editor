## Draft caption
A suspension bridge doesn't stand on legs. The road hangs from cables pulled tight, the towers are pressed
straight down, and huge concrete anchors hold the cable ends. Each Golden Gate main cable is made of 27,572 steel
wires.

## Claims to verify
1. Golden Gate Bridge main span 1,280 m (4,200 ft). Source: Golden Gate Bridge, Highway and Transportation District
   (goldengate.org, "Bridge Design & Construction Statistics").
2. Each of the two Golden Gate main cables is made of 27,572 wires (61 strands of 452 wires), about 92 cm across.
   Same source.
3. A long flat beam that size could not hold its own weight unsupported (the record simple girder spans are a few
   hundred metres, and those are deep continuous girders).
4. In a suspension bridge the deck hangs from vertical suspenders (hangers) on the main cables; cables and hangers
   are in tension; towers carry the cable's vertical load down in compression; anchorages resist the cables' pull at
   the ends (Golden Gate anchorages: about 60,000 tons of concrete each).
5. Golden Gate suspender ropes are spaced 50 ft (15.2 m) apart, so the line says "rows of thin cables", not a spacing.

## Fact check (fresh agent, 2026-11-27)
Nothing wrong; five overstatements fixed: hook card names the Golden Gate (1,280 m is its span, the film's bridge is a
stand-in); "doesn't stand on anything" -> "doesn't stand on legs" (the road rests on the towers and ends); "pulled
tight ... is strong" -> "very strong when you pull it" (a cable is strong pulled, not pushed), with tension arrows also
running back toward the anchors; anchors hold the cables "back", not "down"; "cable" -> "main cable" for 27,572 wires.
Sources: goldengate.org design and construction statistics; goldengate.org cross-section of a main cable; PBS Building
Big, suspension bridge forces.
