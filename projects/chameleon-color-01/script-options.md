# Chameleon color 01: three script options (summaries, for Matthew to pick)

**Topic:** how chameleons change color. **Goal:** reach (default). **Format:** Reel 9:16, animated in Remotion,
colorful flat-cartoon jungle (sample frames in `stills/sample-*.png`). **Reading level:** 3rd grade (sample lines
measure grade 2.5, ~7 words a sentence, `readability.py`). **Runtime:** ~55-60 s (~155 words at 2.8 words/s).

The science all three share (to verify, see below): chameleon color comes partly from pigment (a yellow layer on top,
dark melanin underneath) and mostly, for the fast changes, from a layer of tiny guanine crystals that reflect light.
Relaxed: crystals packed close reflect blue; blue + yellow layer = green. Excited: the chameleon spreads the crystals
apart and they reflect yellow, orange and red. Fast changes are mostly for signals (mood, rivals, mates) and
temperature (darker absorbs more heat); their resting colors do help them blend in.

## A. Myth-buster ("They don't change color to hide")
- **Hook:** "Most people think chameleons change color to hide. But mostly, they change color to talk."
  (Matthew's preferred "Most people think... but actually..." format.)
- **Chain:** what the colors say (red = back off, dark = cold or scared) → so how do they do it? Not paint: tiny
  crystals → close together = green, spread apart = red → recap: crystals move, color changes, the chameleon "talks".
- **Picture:** a calm green chameleon; a rival arrives and both flare red/blue; dive into the skin for the crystals.
- **Strength:** strongest hook, a surprise in the first 3 s. **Risk:** must not say chameleons *never* camouflage.

## B. Tiny mirrors ("A chameleon's skin is full of tiny mirrors")
- **Hook:** "A chameleon's skin is full of tiny crystals. They work like tiny mirrors."
- **Chain:** one beam of sunlight followed into the skin → crystals close together bounce back blue → blue + the yellow
  layer = green → the chameleon gets excited and spreads the crystals → now red bounces back → recap at speed.
- **Picture:** one continuous zoom from the chameleon into a magnifier "lens" of skin layers, the light beam drawn as
  a wave; the same lens shows both states.
- **Strength:** teaches the real mechanism best; one shot fits the motion rules. **Risk:** least emotion; the hook
  is a fact, not a surprise.

## C. A day in Ziggy's life (character story)
- **Hook:** "Meet Ziggy. It's six in the morning, and Ziggy is cold." → "So Ziggy does something cool: Ziggy turns dark."
- **Chain:** 6 a.m. cold → dark soaks up sun · noon a rival → bright "this is MY branch" · inside the skin: crystals
  spread apart = bright colors · evening, calm → back to green · recap through the day.
- **Picture:** a clock in the corner, sky shifting dawn → noon → sunset; Ziggy is the one name used all film.
- **Strength:** most fun for kids; a character to follow. **Risk:** slower hook; the mechanism gets one short beat.

## Recommendation
**A's hook with B's skin dive in the middle** (myth-bust → the crystal lens → back to the rival scene for the recap).
It surprises in 3 s and still shows how the crystals work. C is the pick if the audience is mainly young kids.
