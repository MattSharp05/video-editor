# Chameleon color 01: three script options (v2, modelled on Octopus 01)

**Reference (Matthew, 2026-11-12):** Octopus 01 (`projects/octopus-01/` on branch `claude/octopus-camouflage-video`),
our best-performing video. What we keep from it:
- **Look:** real-time 3D (three.js in Remotion), an original model in a real-feeling world, soft light, haze and grain.
- **One continuous shot** that dives from the animal into its skin (a macro world of cells) and climbs back out.
- **Hook:** something to look at by 0:03; kid words spoken, science names on a centred card ("Iridophores · crystal
  cells"); gold key words in the top caption.
- **Structure:** each layer of skin answers the problem before it, then a twist, a recap through every part, and a
  callback ending ("look twice").
- **Delivery:** AI American male (design-m2) and Floating Cities, ~70 s, no call to action. Reading level about grade 2.

**Topic:** how chameleons change color. **Goal:** reach. **Format:** Reel 9:16, ~65-70 s (~190 words at 2.8 words/s).
Sample lines: grade 1.7, 6.3 words a sentence (`lines_hooks.txt`). Sample frames: `stills/sample-*.png` (3D, same
caption and card style as Octopus 01).

**The science all three share:** green chameleon skin has no green pigment. Under the outer skin is a yellow pigment
layer (xanthophores); under that, cells packed with tiny guanine crystals in a lattice (iridophores); dark melanin cells
at the bottom. Calm: crystals packed close reflect blue; blue light through the yellow layer leaves green. Excited: the
chameleon spreads the crystals apart and they reflect yellow, orange and red. Fast changes are mostly signals (rivals,
mates, stress) and temperature (darker = warmer); the resting colors do help them blend in.

## A. Myth-buster ("They change color to talk")
- **Hook (0-3 s):** "Most people think chameleons change color to hide." / "But mostly, they change color to talk."
  Picture: a green chameleon on a mossy branch; a rival climbs in and both flare. Your favourite format, and it fits
  here because a common belief is wrong.
- **Chain:** what the colors say (bright = back off, dark = I give up or I'm cold) → how? dive into the skin: yellow
  layer, then crystals → packed close = green, spread apart = red → twist: there's no green paint at all → recap,
  back out to the two chameleons; the loser walks away dark.
- **Strength:** surprise and a little story (two rivals) in the first 3 s. **Risk:** must not say they *never* hide.

## B. Watch it change (cold-open demo, closest to Octopus 01's hook)
- **Hook:** "Watch this green chameleon. Now it's red." / "And it has no red paint in its skin."
  Picture: frame 0 green, the colour floods red-orange from the head back by 0:03, like the octopus flashing out of the
  rock.
- **Chain:** no paint, so how? → dive in: yellow layer, crystal layer (card: Iridophores) → packed close bounce back
  blue (+ yellow = green) → spread apart bounce back red → why spread them: a rival, or a cold morning (dark) → recap
  → callback: back to green.
- **Strength:** the demo is the proof, shown before a word of explanation. **Risk:** the reason (talk/warm) gets less
  time.

## C. No green inside ("It's really blue and yellow")
- **Hook:** "This chameleon is green." / "But its skin has no green in it."
  Picture: tight on the turret eye and green scales; on "no green", the green drains out of a patch of skin to show
  yellow over blue.
- **Chain:** then where does green come from? → yellow layer → crystals bounce back blue → blue + yellow = green →
  spread the crystals: red → why they do it → recap → "So next time you see a green chameleon, look again. It's blue
  and yellow."
- **Strength:** the most surprising true claim; ends on a line kids repeat. **Risk:** abstract for the first seconds
  (needs the drain effect to land the hook).

## Recommendation
**B.** It copies the thing that made Octopus 01 work: the viewer *sees* the change at 0:03, before any explanation.
Then fold in A's rivals as the "why" and C's "blue and yellow" as the closing line.
