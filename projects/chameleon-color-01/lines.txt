Most people think chameleons change color to hide.
But mostly, they change color to talk.
This one turns bright. It's saying, back off! This is my branch.
The other one turns dark. That means, okay, you win.
Dark skin can also help a cold chameleon warm up in the sun.
So how does it change color? Here's the strange part. Its green skin has no green paint in it.
Under the clear outer skin is a yellow layer. Under that are tiny crystals in neat rows.
When the chameleon is calm, the crystals sit close together. They bounce back blue light.
The blue light shines up through the yellow layer. And blue and yellow make green!
When it gets excited, its skin cells swell, and the crystals spread apart.
Now they bounce back orange and red light instead.
And to turn dark, dark color spreads up from the bottom layer.
So crystals close together make green. Crystals far apart make red. Dark color from below makes it dark.
Next time a chameleon changes color, it isn't hiding. It's talking.
