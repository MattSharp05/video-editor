# Chameleon color 01: post caption (v2)

Chameleons don't just change color to hide. A lot of the time, they're talking. 🦎

Bright colors say "back off, this is my branch." Dark colors say "okay, you win." And here's the strange part: a green chameleon has no green paint in its skin at all.

Under its skin is a yellow layer, and under that, tiny crystals in neat rows. Packed close together, the crystals bounce back blue light, and blue shining through yellow looks green. When the chameleon gets excited, the crystals spread apart, and the skin glows yellow and orange instead.

Next time you see a chameleon change color, look closely. It might be talking.

🔬 The science: green panther chameleon skin gets its color from guanine nanocrystals in cells called iridophores. They reflect blue light under a layer of yellow pigment. Excited males move the crystals apart, which shifts the reflected light toward longer wavelengths (Teyssier et al. 2015, Nature Communications). Their fast, dramatic color changes evolved mostly for signaling to other chameleons (Stuart-Fox & Moussalli 2008, PLoS Biology).
