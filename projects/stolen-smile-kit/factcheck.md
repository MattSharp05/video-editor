# The Stolen Smile: fact-check of scripts v0.1 (fresh agent, 2026-11-07; rewords applied in v1)

I checked all three scripts against sources I opened myself. None of the lines is plainly false. Seven lines need rewording, five more could be reworded but are acceptable, and everything else holds as worded. No files were edited.

**Biggest risks:**
1. The Louvre's own kids' video and the Smithsonian say Peruggia hid overnight with two helpers, the Lancelotti brothers. Peruggia himself said he walked in alone on Monday morning. Your lines about Monday morning hold under both versions, but Part 1 tells it as a lone-thief story, so the caption needs a caveat.
2. Kafka was not famous in 1911, so "the famous writer Franz Kafka" is wrong as worded.
3. Sources disagree on whether the 500,000 lire was in the letter or asked for in person, and Peruggia later said he set no price.
4. "Secretly brings a museum expert" is wrong. The Uffizi director came openly to check the painting; the secret was that police would be called.
5. "Body size" should be "body measurements" (the Bertillon system). And one version says police never tried to match the print at all.

## Caption caveats

**Part 1:**
- How he got in is disputed. The Louvre's own film, the Smithsonian and Hoobler say he hid overnight in a storeroom; the Louvre and the Smithsonian add two helpers. Peruggia said he walked in alone on Monday morning.
- Simplified: he also unscrewed the doorknob before the plumber came. The theft was on Monday 21 August 1911 and was found the next day.

**Part 2:**
- Apollinaire was held between 3 days and about a week, depending on the source.
- Sources disagree on the thumbprint. One says the files couldn't be searched by fingerprint; another says police never checked his prints.
- Sources disagree on where in his room the painting was kept: a trunk, a table, or a crate.
- Kafka visited in September 1911, before he was famous.
- The signature appears as "Leonardo," "Leonardo V." or "Leonard."

**Part 3:**
- The 500,000-lire demand comes from Geri's account; Peruggia denied naming a price.
- Arrest date: 11 or 12 December 1913.
- His patriotic motive is doubted, since he tried to sell the painting.
- The sentence was 1 year 15 days, cut to about 7 months on appeal; sources say 6, 7 or 8 months.
- The "100,000+ in two days" figure is from the Smithsonian; History.com says at least 120,000.
- Leonardo most likely brought the painting to France in 1516, and Francis I acquired it.
