# Caught by Wireless: picture brainstorm (fresh agent, no production rules)

Sources used: the three `lines.txt` scripts and `wireless-kit/research.md`. Nothing else was read.

Ground rules carried through every idea below:
- No cuts. Each frame grows out of one point of focus in the frame before (marked **→ grows into** at the end of each line).
- No black void. Every frame has a lit, textured world behind it; "nothing but arrows changing" is treated as a failure.
- Never shown or described: the remains, what the drug does, the execution. Cora is warm and lively whenever she appears. Ethel goes free and is never drawn as a villain.
- Kinds of idea used per line: **Literal**, **Analogy** (an everyday comparison), **Wacky** (impossible), **Scale** (a big jump in size), **Personified** (an object with a face or a will), **Kid** (what a ten-year-old would draw). A ★ marks the one I would pick, and says why.

---

## Part 1. Series-wide devices (pick 3–4)

### 1. The spark: one blue-white point that carries the camera
A single crackling wireless spark is the series' point of focus. It is the "dot" of Morse code.
- **Opening of every part.** The screen starts as one spark. It taps out the part title in Morse (dot, dash, dot) and every dot leaves a little burn mark that becomes the letters of "Caught by Wireless".
- **During the part.** Whenever the story jumps place or time, the spark is the thing that travels. It slips down a telegraph wire, runs along a crack in the cellar floor, hops along a ship's rigging, or becomes the glint in Dew's eye. The camera follows it, so the next frame grows out of it with no cut.
- **Payoff.** In Part 1 the spark is tiny and nobody notices it: a glint on Cora's brooch, a gleam on the doctor's glasses. In Part 2 it becomes the real wireless message. In Part 3 it is the "one message sent through the air" of the final line. The whole series turns out to be one spark's journey.

### 2. The runaway mustache (running gag, fun for kids)
- **Part 1.** The doctor's huge mustache has a life of its own. It twitches when he lies, droops when he is nervous, and bristles at Inspector Dew.
- **Part 2.** He shaves it off, and the mustache escapes. It flaps out of the porthole like a little brown bird and follows the ship across the ocean. It perches on the funnel and hides in a lifeboat. In the newspaper's "wanted" picture it is still drawn on, which is exactly why nobody recognises him.
- **Part 3.** When Dew says "Good morning, Doctor Crippen", the mustache lands on the doctor's lip. Caught! It stays perched on the dock rail, in the courtroom gallery, and finally sits on the question mark in the DNA twist, looking puzzled.
- It keeps the doctor readable through the disguise: viewers always know which character is him.

### 3. Cora's feather (warmth, memory, continuity)
- Cora's big hat has one tall ostrich plume. It is her signature, the way she says "I'm here".
- After she vanishes, the plume is what is left of her. It is never sad-gothic, just bright and pink-orange in a grey world. It drifts into the friends' hands, sits on the police desk, and stands upright in the newspaper's hat-band.
- In Part 3 it becomes the court clerk's quill that writes the verdict, and finally the question mark of the twist. At the very end it floats up past the spark into the sky. That is a gentle bow for Cora with no death imagery.

### 4. The living front page, and how each part opens and ends
- A 1910 newspaper, *The Daily Wire*, sits on a tabletop in every part. Its headline retypes itself (letters flip like a station board), its engraving comes alive, and its race map has two toy ships that move.
- **Open.** Each part begins *inside* the last frame of the previous part. The camera pulls back out of the cliffhanger image, and it turns out to be the engraving on today's front page. The "Last time" recap is the newspaper ageing by a day: the headline flips and the old picture shrinks into a corner.
- **End.** Each part ends with the camera diving *into* one object that carries over:
  - Part 1 ends in the empty dark doorway of 39 Hilldrop Crescent. That doorway becomes the ship's porthole that opens Part 2.
  - Part 2 ends on the pilot's cap brim. Part 3 opens by lifting it.
  - Part 3 ends on the spark rising into the sky. That loops the series back to its very first frame.
- **The race map.** The two-ships race map is printed on the front page. It appears small in Part 1 (an ad corner: "Montrose sails Wednesdays"), takes the whole page in Part 2, and in Part 3 is folded into a paper boat.

Recommendation: use all four. The spark is the camera, the mustache is the laugh, the feather is the heart, and the newspaper is the calendar.

## Part 2. Three contrasting overall looks (each different from paper toy theatre)

### Look A. "Stop the Press": a living 1910 engraving ★
- **What it looks like.** Cream newsprint with crosshatch and halftone ink. Characters are drawn as the steel-engraving illustrations from 1910 papers.
- **Colour.** One spot colour per thread: electric cyan for the spark, warm coral for Cora's feather. Everything else is ink on paper.
- **Camera.** It dives *through* halftone dots. The dot pattern turns into rain, sea spray, or the crowd at the Old Bailey. Columns of type slide like scenery, and headlines are architecture. Ink bleeds outwards to draw the next scene.
- **Why it wins.** It is flat-on-flat and graphic, the opposite of a 3D cut-out stage. It is natively "true crime 1910", and the newspaper device comes built in. The background is never black: it is paper.

### Look B. "Marconi Blueprint": the case drafted live
- **What it looks like.** Deep cyan blueprint paper with white chalk-and-ink lines that draw themselves. Every scene is a technical drawing: house cross-sections, ship plans, the wireless set exploded into parts.
- **How it moves.** Characters are white line figures with measurement arrows that turn into motion paths. The spark is the drafting pen's nib.
- **Risk.** It could feel like a presentation. Avoid that by making the drawing itself the action: lines chase each other, and a cross-section of the house slides along like a dolls' house.
- **Best for.** Part 2's wireless and race.

### Look C. "Fog and Lantern": a sepia shadow-play in coloured light
- **What it looks like.** Silhouettes cast on glowing amber, teal and rose magic-lantern backdrops, with drifting London fog and lamplight halos. Ships are lit outlines on a luminous sea.
- **How it moves.** Shadows can do impossible things: Crippen's shadow runs off without him, and Dew's shadow is twice his size. The background is always a lit gradient, never black.
- **Best for.** Suspense. It is the most "movie".
- **Risk.** It is less kid-cartoony than A, and silhouettes make faces (and the disguises) harder to read.

**Pick:** Look A, borrowing Look B's blueprint for one sequence: Part 2's "wireless explained" line, where the newspaper flips to a Marconi diagram. It also borrows Look C's lamplit fog for night scenes.

## Part 3. Picture ideas, line by line

### Part one: the missing singer

**1.1 "In 1910, a woman vanished in London. Then a secret message from a ship helped catch the man accused of her murder."**
- **Literal.** A foggy 1910 London street. A woman's figure with a feathered hat walks into the fog and is gone; only the feather spins in the lamplight. Then a liner far out at sea flashes one spark from its mast.
- **Analogy.** A game of hide and seek: a hand counts on a lamppost "...8, 9, 10", turns around, and nobody's there.
- **Wacky.** The word "VANISHED" is typed onto the newspaper and its letters walk off the page one by one. The last letter is chased by a spark that zips in from the edge of the page.
- **Scale.** Start on the spark, a speck. Pull back: it is on a ship's mast, the ship is a dot on the ocean, the ocean is a map on a front page, and the front page is in a hand in London.
- **Kid.** A map with a dotted line from a little house to a big ship, with a lightning bolt between them.
- ★ **Scale pull-back.** It plants the spark and the newspaper in the first five seconds.
- **→ grows into:** the spark flashes and burns the series title into the paper.

**1.2 "This is Caught by Wireless. Part one: the missing singer."**
- **Literal.** A title card on the front page.
- **Personified.** The spark taps the title out in Morse. Each tap prints a letter, and the subtitle "the missing singer" is a theatre bill with one name scratched out.
- **Wacky.** The letters of the title are caught in a butterfly net made of telegraph wire.
- **Kid.** A spotlight on an empty stage, with a microphone stand and a feather lying on the floor.
- ★ **The Morse spark printing the title.** It returns in all three parts.
- **→ grows into:** the empty spotlight. Cora steps into it.

**1.3 "Cora Crippen was a singer with big hats and lots of friends."**
- **Literal.** Cora on a music-hall stage singing, in a hat so big it needs its own spotlight.
- **Wacky.** Her hat keeps growing as she sings. It becomes a whole garden, then a bird's nest, then a hat with a tiny brass band playing on its brim.
- **Analogy.** Her address book is so full it is bursting at the seams: names spill out and turn into waving friends.
- **Scale.** Her hat brim is wide enough that all her friends stand under it, as if under an umbrella.
- **Kid.** A smiling lady with a hat bigger than her body and stick-figure friends all around.
- ★ **The friends sheltering under the hat brim.** It shows "big hats" and "lots of friends" in one picture, and it is warm. Introduce the feather here.
- **→ grows into:** the hat brim tilts down and casts a shadow, and in the shadow stands a small man.

**1.4 "Her husband was Hawley Crippen, a small, quiet doctor with round glasses and a big mustache."**
- **Literal.** An engraved portrait of a small man with gold-rimmed glasses and a heavy mustache.
- **Personified.** The mustache is the loud one. It bows, twirls and introduces itself, while the doctor behind it says nothing.
- **Scale.** Next to Cora's giant hat he is tiny. The camera has to zoom in to find him, and his round glasses fill the frame.
- **Analogy.** A "build-a-face" kit: glasses click on, then the mustache clips on like a toy accessory. Hold on to this, because Part 2 undoes it.
- **Kid.** Two circles for the glasses and a huge bushy mustache, labelled "the doctor".
- ★ **The build-a-face kit with a mustache that has a mind of its own.** It sets up the disguise gag in Part 2, and kids will spot him later.
- **→ grows into:** the round glasses become two lit windows of a house at night.

**1.5 "One winter night, two friends came over for dinner and cards. When they left, Cora waved goodbye from the front steps."**
- **Literal.** 39 Hilldrop Crescent, a semi-detached Victorian house, lit up on a snowy night. Inside: dinner, then whist. Two friends walk out, and Cora stands at the top of the steps waving.
- **Analogy.** The house as a cuckoo clock: the door opens, the little figures come out on the hour, and Cora waves like the cuckoo.
- **Scale.** A playing card fills the frame, the Queen of Hearts. It flips over and the pattern on its back becomes the street at night.
- **Kid.** A house with smoke from the chimney, snowflakes, and a lady waving with a speech bubble saying "Goodnight!"
- ★ **Literal, but lovely.** Make this Cora's warmest moment, with a big wave and the plume bobbing. The card flip can be the way in. It is the last time we see her, so let it glow.
- **→ grows into:** the door closes, and the lit window in the door's glass shrinks to a point.

**1.6 "Her friends never saw her again."**
- **Literal.** Morning. The same steps, empty, with snow over the footprints.
- **Analogy.** A photo album where every page has Cora, until one page is a hat-shaped gap.
- **Wacky.** The friends hold up a "Have you seen her?" poster, and the drawing on it fades like breath on glass. Only the feather stays in colour.
- **Personified.** The front steps themselves wait, with the doormat looking up hopefully day after day as a calendar flips behind it.
- **Kid.** The house from 1.5 again with nobody in the doorway, and a question mark.
- ★ **The steps waiting while the calendar flips.** Something changes (time) while the absence stays, which fixes the "nothing changes" problem without showing anything sad.
- **→ grows into:** the falling calendar page is caught by the doctor's hand.

**1.7 "Soon, the doctor told them Cora had gone to America."**
- **Literal.** The doctor talking to the friends and pointing west.
- **Wacky.** His mustache acts it out: it mimes a ship, waves "bye bye", and puffs a little steamer across the table.
- **Analogy.** A postcard "From America!" that he holds up, with the paint still wet.
- **Scale.** His pointing finger leads the camera across a globe on the desk: London, the Atlantic, America.
- **Kid.** A little stick-Cora on a big boat with a flag saying "USA".
- ★ **The wet-paint postcard.** Kids instantly read "made up". The drip is the clue.
- **→ grows into:** the postcard flips, and on its back is a black-edged card.

**1.8 "Then he said she got sick there, and died."**
- **Literal.** A death notice in a theatrical paper and a telegram.
- **Analogy.** The story gets a second chapter written in the same wet ink. He writes it on the back of the postcard and blots it in a hurry.
- **Personified.** The mustache droops dramatically and fakes a sob into a hanky. The friends exchange looks.
- **Kid.** A card with a sad face, and the friends with eyebrows raised.
- ★ **The mustache's over-acted fake sob.** It is gentle, slightly comic, and plants doubt. No sickbed and no funeral imagery: it is only a piece of paper.
- **→ grows into:** one friend's raised eyebrow becomes a question mark.

**1.9 "But her friends had questions. Where in America? When was the funeral? The doctor's answers were fuzzy."**
- **Literal.** The friends lean in. He shrugs.
- **Wacky.** Each question is a bright, sharp speech balloon. His answer balloons are out of focus, then fluffy clouds, then pure fog that fills the room.
- **Analogy.** A map of America with a pin he cannot place. The pin wobbles over the whole continent.
- **Scale.** Their three questions grow into giant question marks that tower over him like streetlamps.
- **Kid.** Big question marks over the friends and squiggles over the doctor.
- ★ **Sharp balloons against fuzzy balloons.** "Fuzzy" is the word, so let it be literal-in-a-wacky-way. It reads instantly.
- **→ grows into:** the fog clears to reveal one sparkle, a brooch.

**1.10 "And there was something else. The doctor's typist, Ethel, was wearing Cora's jewelry."**
- **Literal.** A ball at the Music Hall. Ethel dances by in a brooch the friends know.
- **Scale.** Start on the brooch, huge and glinting (the spark hides in it). Pull back to the ball, where one friend's opera glasses are locked onto it.
- **Analogy.** "Spot the difference" between two pictures, Cora in the brooch and Ethel in the brooch.
- **Personified.** The brooch recognises the friends and winks at them.
- **Kid.** A lady with a shiny pin and a friend pointing: "That's Cora's!"
- ★ **The opera-glasses zoom onto the brooch.** It is a natural single point of focus, and the spark first appears hidden in the gem. Draw Ethel as a normal young typist, not a schemer.
- **→ grows into:** the opera glasses' two lenses become the two lamps over the Scotland Yard door.

**1.11 "So Cora's friends went to the police at Scotland Yard."**
- **Literal.** The friends march into Scotland Yard.
- **Analogy.** Kids telling a teacher. Here the "teacher" is a building with a bobby's helmet for a roof.
- **Personified.** Scotland Yard as a big sleepy building that opens one window-eye when they knock.
- **Wacky.** The friends carry Cora's feather like a flag at the head of a little parade.
- **Kid.** A blue police box, which kids may love even though it is anachronistic (avoid; use a bobby's helmet instead).
- ★ **The feather-flag parade into a building that wakes up.** It is warm, and it is the group's loyalty to Cora.
- **→ grows into:** the window-eye opens, and inside it a bowler hat lifts.

**1.12 "Inspector Walter Dew came to ask the doctor some questions."**
- **Literal.** A heavy-set Chief Inspector with a bowler and notebook on the doorstep of Hilldrop Crescent.
- **Scale.** Dew is so big he fills the doorway. The tiny doctor looks up and up.
- **Analogy.** A cat and a mouse, with Dew as a polite, enormous cat in a bowler.
- **Personified.** Dew's notebook flaps open by itself, hungry for answers, and his pencil sharpens itself.
- **Kid.** A big policeman with a magnifying glass.
- ★ **The doorway scale gag.** It sets up the duel: big against small, notebook against mustache.
- **→ grows into:** the notebook page fills with the doctor's words.

**1.13 "This time, the doctor told a new story. He said Cora had left him and run away."**
- **Literal.** The doctor talking while Dew writes.
- **Wacky.** Story number one (the America postcard) is crumpled, and story two (the "ran away" note) is pulled from the same pocket. The pocket is full of spare stories.
- **Analogy.** A choose-your-own-adventure book where he keeps flipping to a different ending.
- **Personified.** The mustache visibly sweats. One hair at a time stands up.
- **Kid.** Two speech bubbles that don't match, with a red X on the first.
- ★ **The pocket of spare stories.** It shows "new story" as a physical object, and it is funny.
- **→ grows into:** the pocket becomes a doorway, and the doctor ushers Dew through it.

**1.14 "He even showed the inspector around his house, all the way down to the cellar."**
- **Literal.** A tour from parlour to stairs to the coal cellar.
- **Scale.** A dolls'-house cross-section. The camera follows the two tiny figures down floor by floor, like an elevator, to the brick-floored cellar.
- **Analogy.** An estate agent's tour, with the doctor being far too helpful: "And here's the cellar!"
- **Personified.** The cellar stairs creak "nothing to see here" in a nervous voice.
- **Kid.** A house cut in half with a dotted path running down to the bottom.
- ★ **The dolls'-house cross-section descent.** Continuous camera movement downwards, no cuts, and it plants the cellar floor visually. Show the floor clean and ordinary.
- **→ grows into:** the camera rises back up and out of the front door.

**1.15 "The inspector left. But when he came back, the doctor and Ethel were gone."**
- **Literal.** Dew knocks again. An empty house and an open wardrobe.
- **Wacky.** The doctor's chair is still spinning, his teacup still steaming, and his hat-peg swings.
- **Analogy.** A magic trick: "Now you see them..." A curtain sweeps across, "...now you don't." Two outlines remain in chalk dust, like a cartoon "poof".
- **Personified.** The house shrugs. Its windows look side to side.
- **Kid.** Two dotted-line people where the doctor and Ethel were.
- ★ **The still-spinning chair and steaming cup.** Kids get "just left!" immediately. It is a classic cartoon beat.
- **→ grows into:** the steam curls down through the floorboards, back to the cellar.

**1.16 "So the police searched the house again. Under the cellar floor, they found human remains."**
- **Literal (safe).** A poker taps the bricks. One brick wobbles loose. We never see below: the camera stays on the police faces, lit from below, hats coming off.
- **Analogy.** A floor tile in a game that sounds hollow: "tok, tok, TONK".
- **Scale.** Start on the single brick, huge. Pull back to the cellar, then the house, then the street, then the newspaper headline "CELLAR DISCOVERY" slamming down.
- **Personified.** The brick that has been "holding its breath" all along finally lets out a sigh.
- **Kid.** A policeman pointing at the floor, with a big "!" above him.
- ★ **The poker test plus the faces lit from below, rising into the headline slam.** It is all suspense and no content. "Human remains" is heard in the voiceover only, and the frame shows the newspaper.
- **→ grows into:** the newspaper's portrait of the doctor, with an empty face-hole.

**1.17 "And the doctor? He had vanished."**
- **Literal.** A "WANTED" poster with his portrait.
- **Wacky.** The doctor's portrait on the poster steps out of its frame and tiptoes off the page, leaving a blank oval.
- **Scale.** The camera rushes through the blank oval into the dark, and finds a ship's porthole far away with a spark at its rim.
- **Kid.** A "WANTED" poster with a big question mark for a face.
- ★ **The portrait tiptoeing out of the poster, then the dive through the empty oval.** It ends with a laugh and a chill together, and the oval becomes Part 2's porthole.
- **→ grows into (Part 2 opening):** the porthole.

### Part two: the chase

**2.1 "Last time, Cora Crippen vanished, and police found human remains under her cellar floor. Her husband had run away."**
- **Literal.** Yesterday's front page.
- **Device.** Start inside the porthole (Part 1's last frame). Pull back: it is the engraving on *The Daily Wire*. The day's date flips, and three small panels replay Part 1: the feather, the brick, the empty poster.
- **Wacky.** The newspaper rewinds like a flip-book.
- **Kid.** Three boxes like a comic strip: lady, floor, running man.
- ★ **The front page pull-back plus three-panel recap.** It is quick and it shows the continuity. The cellar panel shows the brick only.
- **→ grows into:** the spark taps the title.

**2.2 "This is Caught by Wireless. Part two: the chase."**
- **Same device.** The spark taps the title in Morse. "the chase" is printed and immediately starts running: the letters sprint across the page.
- **Kid.** Racing ships with speed lines.
- ★ **Morse spark, with the letters running.**
- **→ grows into:** the letters run into a printing press.

**2.3 "Police sent out his description, and newspapers printed his picture."**
- **Literal.** Telegraph wires buzzing to ports. A printing press spits out his face.
- **Scale.** One portrait copies itself: two, four, a thousand. They fill the city, cover walls and trams, and become a mosaic of the doctor's face.
- **Wacky.** His face is on everything: milk bottles, kites, a dog's coat, a pigeon.
- **Analogy.** A "missing cat" poster, multiplied by a million.
- **Kid.** Lots of little drawings of the same mustache face.
- ★ **The multiplying portrait mosaic.** It shows "everywhere" in one move, and it keeps the mustache prominent, so the next line can undo it.
- **→ grows into:** one portrait in the mosaic is a mirror, and the real doctor is shaving in it.

**2.4 "But the doctor had shaved off his big mustache and stopped wearing his glasses."**
- **Literal.** Shaving in the mirror.
- **Runaway-mustache gag.** The razor approaches, the mustache panics, and then it hops off his face and flies out of the window like a bird. The glasses fold themselves and walk into a drawer.
- **Analogy.** The "build-a-face kit" from 1.4 run in reverse: parts clicking off.
- **Kid.** Before-and-after drawings with a big arrow between them.
- ★ **The mustache escaping as a bird.** This is the start of the running gag, and the payoff of 1.4.
- **→ grows into:** the bird-mustache flutters past a second mirror, where scissors snip.

**2.5 "And Ethel cut her hair short and dressed as a boy."**
- **Literal.** Ethel snips her hair and puts on a boy's suit and cap.
- **Analogy.** A dress-up box, with the suit too big and the cap needing a hanky stuffed inside it (from Kendall's account).
- **Wacky.** Each snip turns a lock of hair into a ribbon that floats away.
- **Kid.** A girl with a cap pulled down and a drawn-on "?"
- ★ **The dress-up box, with the stuffed cap and baggy sleeves.** It is playful, not sinister, and it plants the clue the captain will spot.
- **→ grows into:** the two disguised figures step towards a gangway.

**2.6 "They called themselves Mr. Robinson and his son, and got on a big ship to Canada."**
- **Literal.** Antwerp dock. "John Robinson, 55, merchant" and "John C. Robinson, 16, student" sign the passenger list. The Montrose is a single-funnel liner.
- **Scale.** The two figures are ants at the foot of the giant hull. The camera climbs the side of the ship to the funnel.
- **Personified.** The ship's nameplate "MONTROSE" raises an eyebrow at them as they board.
- **Analogy.** A name tag at a party: "Hello, my name is ROBINSON", stuck on wonky.
- **Kid.** A huge boat with two tiny people and a "To Canada" arrow.
- ★ **The camera climb up the hull from the tiny pair to the funnel.** It is continuous, big and cinematic. The wonky name tag can be a detail on the way.
- **→ grows into:** at the top of the ship, a captain's cap.

**2.7 "The ship's captain was named Henry Kendall. He watched his passengers closely."**
- **Literal.** The captain on the bridge with a telescope.
- **Scale.** Look *through* his telescope. The circle sweeps the deck past passenger after passenger, and stops.
- **Personified.** The captain's eyebrows are binoculars. Or his telescope extends absurdly long along the deck.
- **Analogy.** A teacher who sees everything from the front of the class.
- **Kid.** A captain with one huge eye.
- ★ **The telescope circle sweeping the deck.** It is literally a single point of focus that guides the viewer, which is exactly what the creator wants.
- **→ grows into:** the circle stops on two pairs of hands.

**2.8 "The captain later said the boy's clothes didn't fit, and the two held hands like a couple."**
- **Literal.** The "son" in baggy trousers, with "father" and "son" squeezing hands.
- **Wacky.** The trousers are so baggy a seagull nests in them. The cap slips, and the hanky inside pokes out.
- **Analogy.** A spot-the-mistake puzzle: little red circles pop up on each clue (the trousers, the cap, the hands).
- **Personified.** A little heart pops up over their hands, and they swat it away.
- **Kid.** Two people holding hands with a heart over them and "dad??" written beside.
- ★ **Spot-the-mistake circles popping on the clues.** The viewer gets to play detective with the captain.
- **→ grows into:** the red circles slide together into one bulb, a lightbulb over the captain's head.

**2.9 "The captain had read about the missing doctor. He wondered, could this be him?"**
- **Literal.** The captain holds the newspaper's "wanted" portrait up next to Mr. Robinson.
- **Wacky.** The captain draws a mustache onto the real doctor in his imagination, and the runaway mustache (perched on the rail) flies over and tries itself on. Perfect fit.
- **Analogy.** A cut-out mask matched against a face, aligning like a puzzle.
- **Kid.** A thought bubble with the wanted poster inside.
- ★ **The mustache flying back to try itself on.** It pays off the gag, and the match "clicks" visually.
- **→ grows into:** the click becomes a crackle, a spark at the top of the mast.

**2.10 "His ship had a new invention called wireless. It could send messages through the air, with no wires at all."**
- **Literal.** The Marconi room: sparks, a Morse key, earphones, the operator Lawrence Hughes.
- **Analogy.** Two tin-can telephones, but someone cuts the string and it *still works*.
- **Wacky.** Messages as little paper birds flying off the ship's antenna.
- **Scale.** Dive into the spark gap. The newspaper flips into the blueprint look: a Marconi diagram that draws itself, and the spark is a giant lightning bolt that rides an invisible wave out over the sea.
- **Personified.** The spark is a tiny excited character finally getting its big moment.
- **Kid.** A ship with zigzag lightning lines coming off the top.
- ★ **Tin-can phones with the string cut, then the spark diving into the blueprint wave.** The analogy makes "no wires" instant for kids, and the blueprint wave keeps moving, so it is not a static diagram.
- **→ grows into:** the operator's finger lands on the Morse key.

**2.11 "So the captain sent a secret message back to land. It said the doctor might be on board, with a helper dressed as a boy."**
- **Literal.** The key taps, and the message crackles off the antenna. The text is shown short: "CRIPPEN ON BOARD? ACCOMPLICE DRESSED AS BOY."
- **Scale.** Follow the message: the spark leaps from the mast, skims wave tops, and the camera rides it all the way to land, to a receiving set in Liverpool and a messenger running to Scotland Yard.
- **Analogy.** Passing a secret note in class, over the heads of the whole class (here, the ocean).
- **Wacky.** The Morse dots and dashes march across the sea like little ducks in a row.
- **Kid.** A long zigzag line from the ship to a police station.
- ★ **Riding the spark from ship to land.** This is the series' signature move and a single continuous line across the ocean.
- **→ grows into:** the message lands on Dew's desk, and he grabs his hat.

**2.12 "Inspector Dew got the news. He jumped on a faster ship to beat them to Canada."**
- **Literal.** Dew runs up the gangway of the Laurentic in Liverpool.
- **Wacky.** Dew leaps from the dock straight onto the departing ship's deck in one cartoon jump, holding onto his bowler.
- **Analogy.** The tortoise and the hare, with two ships, and the hare is the good guy this time.
- **Scale.** Pull up and out from Dew's jump to a bird's-eye map. Both ships appear on the newspaper race map.
- **Kid.** Two boats, one with "FAST" written on it.
- ★ **The cartoon leap that rises into the race map.** It is action plus geography in one move.
- **→ grows into:** the race map is printed on the front page.

**2.13 "Soon, newspapers all over the world were writing about the chase."**
- **Literal.** Papers in many languages with the race map and headlines.
- **Scale.** The front page multiplies across the globe. The camera spins over a world where every city is holding up the same page.
- **Wacky.** Newsboys on every continent shout at once, and their shouts become a wave that rolls round the globe.
- **Personified.** The newspapers flap like a flock of birds migrating across the ocean after the ships.
- **Kid.** A globe with lots of tiny newspapers stuck on it.
- ★ **The flock of newspapers following the ships.** It is motion, it links to the race, and it is memorable.
- **→ grows into:** the flock flies over the Montrose's deck, but none land.

**2.14 "Everyone knew, except the doctor, out at sea with no news."**
- **Literal.** The doctor relaxes in a deck chair, reading a novel.
- **Analogy.** A surprise party where everyone knows except the guest of honour, here with the whole world behind the sofa.
- **Wacky.** The newspaper flock passes right over his head while he looks the other way. His own escaped mustache sits next to him, frantically trying to tell him.
- **Scale.** A split frame grows out of one line: the giant noisy world above, and the tiny calm deck chair below.
- **Kid.** The doctor with headphones on, and a crowd shouting "!!!" behind him.
- ★ **The surprise-party analogy, with the mustache trying to warn him.** It is funny, it is dramatic irony, and kids get it right away.
- **→ grows into:** his novel's page turns, and the page becomes the sea.

**2.15 "For days, the two ships raced across the sea."**
- **Literal.** Two liners pushing through waves, days and nights passing.
- **Wacky.** The race map comes alive as a board game. A giant hand rolls dice for each ship and they hop square by square, with the Laurentic getting a "move ahead 3" card.
- **Scale.** Huge on the waves, then tiny as dots on the map, then huge again, so the camera keeps zooming through the race.
- **Analogy.** Two runners on a running track with a calendar in place of a lap counter.
- **Kid.** Two boats with smoke puffs and a finish flag drawn on Canada.
- ★ **The board-game race map, intercut by zooming (not cutting) to the real ships.** This is the fix for the creator's "moon and tide arrows" complaint: the map is never just arrows, because something always moves, overtakes or rolls.
- **→ grows into:** the Laurentic's token reaches a lighthouse square first.

**2.16 "Then one morning, near Canada, a small boat came out to meet the doctor's ship."**
- **Literal.** Dawn at Father Point: a lighthouse, the St Lawrence, and a little pilot boat rowing out to the big liner.
- **Scale.** A tiny boat against a giant liner, like a mouse approaching an elephant.
- **Personified.** The lighthouse's beam winks at the little boat: "Go on, now."
- **Kid.** A lighthouse, a sunrise, and a little boat with a big ship.
- ★ **The lighthouse-wink, tiny boat against huge liner.** It is a beautiful dawn colour moment. Use warm light here, never black.
- **→ grows into:** the tiny boat bumps the hull, and a rope ladder drops.

**2.17 "A pilot climbed aboard. But he wasn't really a pilot."**
- **Literal.** A man in a pilot's cap and coat climbs the ladder.
- **Wacky.** As he climbs, his shadow on the hull is wearing a bowler hat.
- **Personified.** The runaway mustache, perched on the rail, sees him and flies off squawking.
- **Scale.** End on an extreme close-up of the cap's brim. Freeze there.
- **Kid.** A man in a cap, with a hidden police badge drawn under his coat.
- ★ **The bowler-hat shadow on the hull, then the push in to the cap brim.** It is the cliffhanger, and the brim is Part 3's first frame.
- **→ grows into (Part 3 opening):** the cap brim.

### Part three: the verdict

**3.1 "Last time, Inspector Dew raced across the ocean, and a pilot climbed onto the doctor's ship."**
- **Device.** Start on the cap brim (Part 2's last frame). Pull back to the front page, with the date flipping and the race map showing both ships at Father Point. A two-panel recap: the Laurentic overtaking, the ladder.
- ★ **Same pull-back grammar as 2.1.**
- **→ grows into:** the spark taps the title.

**3.2 "This is Caught by Wireless. Part three: the verdict."**
- **Device.** The spark's Morse prints the title. "the verdict" is stamped by a gavel, and the stamp sends a ripple through the paper.
- ★ **Morse plus gavel stamp.**
- **→ grows into:** the ripple lifts the pilot's cap.

**3.3 "The pilot was really Inspector Dew, in disguise."**
- **Literal.** He lifts the cap, and it's Dew.
- **Wacky.** Dew peels off the pilot outfit like a banana, revealing his tweed and bowler underneath. The bowler pops up from inside the cap like a jack-in-the-box.
- **Analogy.** The reveal at the end of a Scooby-Doo episode, with the mask pulled off, but the good guy is underneath.
- **Kid.** "Pilot = Dew" with an equals sign.
- ★ **The jack-in-the-box bowler.** It is a pop of surprise, and it echoes how Dew was drawn in Part 1.
- **→ grows into:** Dew walks forward with the camera at his shoulder, down the deck.

**3.4 "He walked up to Mr. Robinson and said, "Good morning, Doctor Crippen.""**
- **Literal.** In the captain's cabin, face to face. (Note: the research says the arrest was in the captain's cabin and the exact wording is disputed. The line's wording is fine.)
- **Scale.** Dew's shadow stretches down the deck and reaches the doctor before Dew does.
- **Runaway-mustache gag.** On "Doctor Crippen", the mustache flies in and lands back on his lip. Caught!
- **Analogy.** A game of tag: "Tag, you're it."
- **Kid.** A speech bubble, "Good morning, Doctor Crippen", and the doctor's eyes as big as plates.
- ★ **The mustache landing back on his lip.** It is the funniest beat of the series, and the gag pays off at the exact moment of capture.
- **→ grows into:** the doctor's widened eyes become two portholes.

**3.5 "The chase was over. Police found Ethel too, still dressed as a boy."**
- **Literal.** Ethel in another cabin, with short hair and the boy's suit.
- **Analogy.** The end of hide and seek. The "base" bell rings, and the board-game ships both reach the finish square.
- **Wacky.** The race map's two ship tokens bump together and stop, and a finish flag pops up.
- **Kid.** "THE END (of the chase)" on the map.
- ★ **The board game ending with the flag popping up, flowing into Ethel in the porthole.** Keep Ethel calm and sympathetic, not dragged.
- **→ grows into:** the race map's dotted line reverses back across the ocean.

**3.6 "The doctor and Ethel were taken back to London."**
- **Literal.** The Megantic sailing back to Liverpool.
- **Wacky.** The race map plays in reverse, with the ships rewinding across the ocean like a tape.
- **Scale.** The ocean shrinks to a puddle that the ship crosses in one step, and London grows up around it.
- **Kid.** A boat going the other way, with an arrow back to "London".
- ★ **The map rewinding.** It is a quick way to show "back", and it is continuous.
- **→ grows into:** the dotted line ends at a building with a gold figure on top, the Old Bailey.

**3.7 "The doctor said he didn't do it. So a big trial began at London's famous Old Bailey court."**
- **Literal.** The Old Bailey's dome with the gold statue of Justice holding her scales. Inside, a packed courtroom.
- **Personified.** The statue of Justice on the dome comes alive, lifts her scales and peers down at the case.
- **Scale.** Down from the statue's scales, into the dome, into the courtroom, and to the tiny doctor in the dock.
- **Analogy.** Weighing scales at a market: each piece of evidence will be weighed.
- **Kid.** A judge in a huge wig and a hammer.
- ★ **The statue's scales come alive and the camera descends.** The scales become the visual engine of the trial: each clue drops onto a pan.
- **→ grows into:** a small bottle drops onto the scales.

**3.8 "A scientist found traces of a rare drug in the remains. The doctor had bought that same drug that winter."**
- **Literal (safe).** A scientist with a microscope and a little labelled bottle. The chemist's poisons book with the doctor's signature on a January date.
- **Analogy.** Matching a receipt: a shop's receipt book line and the bottle's label slide together and click.
- **Scale.** Inside the microscope eyepiece, a few glinting specks (no tissue, just abstract sparkles). They match the sparkles in the bottle.
- **Personified.** The shop's poisons book flips open to the right page by itself and points at the signature.
- **Kid.** A bottle with "?" and a receipt with a tick.
- ★ **The receipt and bottle clicking together, dropping onto the scales.** No effects and no body, only "bought" plus "found". Never show it being used.
- **→ grows into:** the scale's pan dips. A scrap of striped cloth drifts down onto the other side.

**3.9 "And with the remains was a piece of pajama shirt from a store where the doctor shopped."**
- **Literal (safe).** A clean scrap of striped cloth with the label "Jones Brothers (Holloway)". Next to it, the shop.
- **Analogy.** A jigsaw: the scrap fits into a pair of pajama trousers in his drawer that had no top.
- **Wacky.** The pajama trousers, left behind in the house, wave their legs: "That's my shirt!"
- **Scale.** Zoom into the label until the letters are as big as the shop front.
- **Kid.** Striped pajamas with one missing piece.
- ★ **The pajama trousers recognising their missing top.** It is funny and it shows the clue clearly. Keep the cloth clean, just a scrap with a label.
- **→ grows into:** the scales tip right down. Twelve jurors lean in.

**3.10 "The jury said the doctor was guilty of murder. He was put to death, which was the law back then."**
- **Literal (safe).** The foreman stands. The newspaper headline flips: "GUILTY".
- **Device.** Cora's feather becomes the clerk's quill and writes the verdict.
- **Analogy.** The last page of a storybook closing.
- **Scale.** Pull far back: the Old Bailey, then London, then a calendar page for 1910 turning to November and gently closing. No prison, no rope, no gallows.
- **Kid.** A judge's hammer and "GUILTY" written big.
- ★ **The feather-quill writes "guilty", then the calendar page closes softly.** "Put to death" is in the voiceover only. The picture is a closed page, which is tactful and still clear.
- **→ grows into:** the calendar page folds, and from behind it a door opens into sunlight.

**3.11 "Ethel had a trial too. She was found not guilty, and went free."**
- **Literal.** Ethel walks out of the court doors into daylight.
- **Analogy.** A bird let out of a cage. Better still, the dress-up box from Part 2 closes, and she walks away in her own clothes.
- **Scale.** The doors open, and the light floods the whole frame, warm.
- **Kid.** A smiling lady and a big "NOT GUILTY" sign with a sun.
- ★ **Doors opening to warm light, in her own clothes.** It makes the acquittal feel as real as the verdict, and it does not hint she "got away with it".
- **→ grows into:** in the sky above her, the spark blinks.

**3.12 "It was one of the first times a radio message from a ship helped catch someone."**
- **Literal.** A museum case: the Marconi set with a plaque.
- **Scale.** Pull back from the spark: today's sky full of signals, from phones and satellites. The 1910 spark was the first star in that constellation.
- **Personified.** The spark gets a tiny medal pinned on it.
- **Kid.** A trophy that says "1st".
- ★ **The spark as the first star of a modern sky of signals.** It is "why it matters" in one picture, and it is a change of scale.
- **→ grows into:** one star in that sky flickers oddly.

**3.13 "But the story has one last twist."**
- **Literal.** The newspaper turns its page.
- **Wacky.** The whole front page twists like a corkscrew and spins us into the future. Typewriters turn into computers as it twists.
- **Kid.** A big curly arrow.
- ★ **The page corkscrewing forward to 2007.** It is literal "twist", and it travels in time.
- **→ grows into:** the twist untwists into a DNA double helix, made of newsprint.

**3.14 "In 2007, scientists tested a tiny sample kept from the trial. They said it did not match Cora's family."**
- **Literal (safe).** A modern lab, a sealed old glass slide in an archive box, and a scientist in gloves.
- **Analogy.** A family tree: three present-day relatives' leaves light up, and the sample's leaf tries to attach, but it doesn't fit, like a wrong jigsaw piece.
- **Scale.** Zoom into the slide until it is a frosted window, behind which is a fuzzy question mark.
- **Kid.** A family tree with one piece with an X on it.
- ★ **The jigsaw piece that won't fit the family tree.** It is clear and gentle, and it never shows tissue. The family tree also gives Cora a family, which adds warmth.
- **→ grows into:** a second hand reaches in and turns the piece around: "maybe it does?"

**3.15 "Other experts say that test may be wrong. The case has never been reopened."**
- **Literal.** Experts arguing over papers.
- **Analogy.** Two experts playing tug-of-war with the jigsaw piece.
- **Personified.** The case file box has a padlock that yawns and stays shut.
- **Scale.** The tug-of-war rope stretches back from 2007 to 1910, and the case box sits sealed in the middle.
- **Kid.** Two people with "Yes!" and "No!" bubbles, and a locked box.
- ★ **Tug-of-war over the piece, with the padlocked box that won't open.** It is balanced: it doesn't say the test proves anything.
- **→ grows into:** the tug-of-war rope bends into a question mark.

**3.16 "Did the doctor do it? A jury said yes. Some people still argue."**
- **Literal.** A question mark over the doctor's portrait.
- **Device.** The question mark is Cora's feather, curled. The runaway mustache perches on the dot and looks puzzled.
- **Analogy.** A scale that balances, then wobbles, then balances, and never quite settles.
- **Kid.** A big "?" with the mustache on it.
- ★ **The feather question mark with the puzzled mustache.** Both running devices meet in one image, which is great for continuity and very memorable.
- **→ grows into:** the feather lifts off from the question mark and floats up.

**3.17 "And it all came down to one captain, one hunch, and one message sent through the air."**
- **Literal.** A montage that grows from one thing to the next with no cuts. The feather floats past the captain's telescope ("one captain"), past the lightbulb over his head ("one hunch"), and past the spark leaping from the mast ("one message").
- **Scale.** The spark rises through the sky and becomes a single star. The camera holds as it flickers dot-dash-dot, which is the first frame of Part 1.
- **Kid.** One captain, one lightbulb, one lightning bolt in a row.
- ★ **The rising feather passing the three "ones", ending in the spark star that loops back to Part 1.** It is one continuous upward line, a gentle send-off for Cora, and it rewards viewers who rewatch the series.

## Part 4. Notes on the creator's complaints, and how the picks answer them

- **"No cuts... a single point of focus that grows."** Every line ends with a **→ grows into** handoff. The recurring carriers are the spark, the feather, the mustache, the telescope circle and the newspaper.
- **"Black background feels like a presentation."** Look A is always newsprint, the nights are lamplit fog, and the dawn at Father Point is warm. No void anywhere.
- **"Moon-and-tide arrows... nothing changes."** The two danger spots are the race map (2.12–2.15) and the wireless explanation (2.10). In both, something must move, overtake, hop, rewind or crackle the whole time: a board-game race, a flock of newspapers, a spark riding a wave. Avoid static diagrams with arrows that blink on and off.
- **"Continuity between the 3."**
  - Each part opens inside the last part's final frame. The runaway mustache runs across all three parts. The feather starts as Cora's hat, becomes the quill, then the question mark, then the star.
  - The newspaper ages one day per part.
  - The spark grows from a hidden glint (Part 1) into the hero of the chase (Part 2) and the final star (Part 3).
