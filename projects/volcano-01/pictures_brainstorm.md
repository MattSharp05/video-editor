# Picture brainstorm (fresh agent, 2026-11-07; script + Matthew's stated rules only)
Tags: L literal, A analogy, W wacky, S scale, P personified, K kid-drawn. ★ = the brainstormer's pick.

1 "Watch this volcano ooze. Then watch this mountain blow its top off." — L: real Kilauea, lava creeps; follow a glow over the Pacific, it rises as St. Helens' snowy cone, which bursts. ★A: two soda bottles, one opened slowly (hiss), one shaken (spray); they become the two volcanoes. W: two volcanoes at a dinner table; one burps, the other's head pops off like a cork. S: ant's-eye split ground: red carpet of lava one side, a bus-sized rock flying over on the other.
2 "The difference is gas, hiding inside the hot melted rock." — L: ground turns see-through, glowing rock full of tiny silver specks. ★W: specks playing hide-and-seek, eyes blinking; push in on one. K: crayon volcano with GAS in a speech bubble that pops.
3 "Melted rock is called magma. Magma holds gas, like the fizz in a soda." — L: slow orange magma pool with heat shimmer. ★A: the magma pool is a giant upside-down soda bottle buried in the Earth; the cap is the volcano's top. S: pull back from one drop to a city-sized sea. P: sleepy dragon with gas in its belly, mouth shut.
4 "Down deep, the heavy rock above squeezes the gas in." — L: rock layers stack above, bubbles shrink to pinpoints. ★A: kids piling on a beach ball, each kid becomes a rock layer. W: elephants stacked on a soda can. S: 10-mile rock stack vs Eiffel Towers.
5 "As magma rises, the squeeze gets weaker. So the gas makes bubbles." — L: magma climbs a crack, layers peel away above, bubbles swell and multiply. ★A: kids climb off the ball, it puffs back up. A: a diver's bubble growing as it rises. P: specks stretch and yawn, puff into bubbles.
6 "What happens next depends on the magma." — ★L/W: the crack forks like a road: pipes to Hawaii and to Washington; camera hesitates. A: juice and honey glasses, a bubble about to drop into each. K: arrow with a question mark growing into the fork.
7 "In Hawaii, the magma is runny. Bubbles rise right through it and pop. The gas gets out a little at a time." — L: Kilauea's lava lake, bubbles race up and burst in small sprays. ★A: straw blowing bubbles in juice. P: bubbles down a waterslide out an EXIT door, waving. S: bubble's-eye ride up through clear orange liquid into the air. W: polite "excuse me" burps.
8 "So the lava pours out in glowing rivers." — L: glowing rivers down Kilauea's real slopes. ★A: syrup over pancakes = the island. S: from orbit down to a wrinkling lava-crust skin. K: crayon squiggles.
9 "Other magma has more silica, the stuff glass is made of. That makes it thick and sticky, like cold honey." — L: sand grains melt into pale stiff magma. ★A: spoon standing up in cold honey; jar tips, nothing comes out. W: giant glassblower blows magma into a vase. P: silica chains holding hands, too tangled to flow.
10 "Bubbles get stuck inside. They push harder and harder." — L: bubbles stall, packed, wobbling. ★A: bubble in honey like a fly in amber, pushing. P: bubbles in a jammed elevator. S: balloon filling in a lunchbox, lid bulging. W: corked kettle whistling louder.
11 "That is what happened at Mount Saint Helens in 1980. Sticky magma pushed the side of the mountain out." — L: real St. Helens, north side bulging. ★A: a thumb pressing a balloon from inside. S: tiny ranger cabin and a time-lapse 1980 clock, bulge growing ~5 ft/day. P: mountain puffing its cheek.
12 "Then an earthquake shook it, and the side slid away." — L: the real north face collapses, biggest landslide recorded. ★A: shaken Jell-O, slab slides off. W: the side opens like a door. K: zigzag shake, cake slice slides off.
13 "All that trapped gas burst out at once. It was like opening a shaken soda." — L: sideways blast of grey ash rips out. ★A: the shaken bottle from 1 loses its cap; spray matches and blends with the volcano's. P: elevator bubbles stampede out cheering.
14 "Ash shot up fifteen miles in fifteen minutes." — L: rising ash column. ★S: column passes markers: skyscraper, jet height (~7 mi), twice that; a 15-minute clock races. A: three Everests. W: school bus riding the column.
15 "The top of the mountain was gone." — L: same terrain before/after; old summit as a ghost wireframe over the crater. ★A: ice cream cone with the scoop bitten off (horseshoe). S: 1,300 ft = four Statues of Liberty fading.
16 "Runny magma lets gas out. It oozes. Sticky magma traps gas. It explodes." — L: both volcanoes side by side with their bubble behaviour. ★A: juice+straw vs honey+stuck bubble, each glass becomes its volcano. K: crayon OOZE / BOOM. W: the two bottles take a bow.

Linking devices: (1) ★ Fizz, one gas bubble we follow the whole way: squeezed small deep down, swells as it rises, splits at the fork; one twin leaves gently at Kilauea, the other gets stuck in the honey and finally blasts 15 miles up. (2) The soda bottle that is the Earth: push in through the plastic, fizz becomes magma, cap becomes each volcano's top; pull back at the end to two bottles on a table. (3) One camera path from Hawaii to Washington: dive into Kilauea, travel under the Pacific floor, rise up St. Helens' pipe.
Fact notes from the brainstormer: at St. Helens the blast went sideways first, the column rose after; "silica is what sand is mostly made of".
