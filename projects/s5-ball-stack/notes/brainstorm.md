# Picture brainstorm (fresh agent, 2026-11-19; brief from animated-video direct.md, no production rules)

Draft lines: 1 "Drop a basketball with a tennis ball sitting on top. How high does the tennis ball go?" 2 "Back to your
hand? Twice as high? Pause and guess." 3 reveal "It flies over the rim." 4 "The basketball hits the floor first and
bounces back up, while the tennis ball is still falling." 5 "So they crash head-on. The tennis ball bounces off a ball
that's already rushing up at it." 6 "It leaves at about three times the speed it fell." 7 "Three times the speed means
nine times the height, in a perfect bounce." 8 "Real balls lose some bounce, but it still flies way above where you
let go." 9 "Try it outside."

1. Hook: literal (hand holds the stack at shoulder height against a tall wall, chalk line at release height);
   personified (tennis ball as a jockey with a seatbelt); a cat dozing on a shelf above the line ("will it reach the
   cat?"); change of scale (low floor camera, basketball as a planet with a moon on top); kid's crayon drawing.
2. Guess: paper bunting flags pop out of the wall at each guess height, a "?" flag slides off the top; the wall becomes
   a carnival high-striker; freeze with a giant pause button; lift panel with floor buttons; crayon checkboxes.
3. Reveal (an event): slow motion, the tennis ball rockets up tearing through the guess flags, clears the rim, net
   flutters; high-striker bell snaps off; ball blows past the cat; camera whip-tilts and overshoots, finds the ball
   against the sky, the hand line a tiny mark below; personified "WAAAH"; kid tapes on more paper.
4. Floor first: strobe ghosts (basketball ghosts turn upward, tennis ghosts keep stepping down); handbrake U-turn;
   zoom into the millimetre gap; staircase analogy; equal up/down arrows in a freeze frame.
5. Head-on: racket analogy (dropped onto a still racket = plop; onto a racket swinging up = THWACK; the basketball is
   the racket); tennis-ball cam with a "closing speed 2v" speedometer; monster truck bumper (heavy hardly slows);
   arrows nose to nose fuse into 2v; crayon BONK.
6. Three times: speed bricks (one falls in, two flip up, the basketball adds its own: three leave); moving walkway;
   strongman catapult; speedometer v -> 3v; crayon speed lines.
7. Nine times: a 1x1 tile snaps open into a 3x3 grid of nine tiles, which stack into a column nine drop-heights tall
   (shows the squaring); braking distance; totem of nine hands; vacuum chamber for "perfect"; fuel gauge.
8. Real loss: tiles crumble off the 9-tall column as puffs of heat, settling at ~3-5, still far above the hand line;
   thermal camera warm spots at the hits; crayon "9" crossed out; high-striker puck stops at 4.
9. Outro: the ball falls back into the viewer's hand; ceiling lamp dented indoors; ball rolls the basketball to camera.

Giant devices: (1) the height tower: one tall wall ruled in drop-heights 1-9, guesses pinned to it, the reveal a tilt
up it, tiles stack on it, loss knocks tiles off; (2) tennis-ball cam with speedometer and altimeter; (3) the carnival
high-striker. Accuracy: racket, walkway, braking and truck analogies match the physics; show the real result at
~3-5x, not 9x; "over the rim" works from ~1.3 m with 3-5x.
