## Draft caption
This shrimp punches so fast the water bursts. The peacock mantis shrimp's club hits about 80 km/h; the water in
front of it tears into vapour bubbles, and when they collapse they hit the shell again, with a flash too faint to see.

## Claims to verify
- Peacock mantis shrimp (Odontodactylus scyllarus) club peak speed ~23 m/s = ~83 km/h (Patek, Korff & Caldwell 2004,
  Nature 428:819; Patek & Caldwell 2005 J Exp Biol 208:3655).
- One of the fastest strikes of any animal (same; among the fastest animal movements, by acceleration ~10,400 g).
- Cavitation bubbles form between the striking club and the target; their collapse makes a second force peak, so the
  prey is hit twice (Patek & Caldwell 2005).
- The collapse emits light (sonoluminescence) and heat; too faint and brief to see without instruments
  (Patek & Caldwell 2005; Wikipedia "Mantis shrimp").

## Fact check (fresh agent, 2026-11-28)
- Fixed: "swings at eighty" -> "can hit up to eighty" (Patek et al. 2004 measured 14-23 m/s; 23 m/s = 83 km/h is the
  peak). Card UP TO 80 KM/H.
- Fixed: "The water in front of the club can't get out of the way" described squeezed, high-pressure water; cavitation
  forms under LOW pressure (fast flow and the club's rebound; Patek & Caldwell 2005). Now: "The club moves so fast, it
  pulls the water apart." Picture: water stretched thin, bubbles at or just after contact, not before it.
- Fixed: "Each one even makes a tiny flash of light and heat": light was not measured for mantis shrimp (snapping
  shrimp: Lohse et al. 2001, Nature 413:477; Patek & Caldwell 2005 mention heat and luminescence of collapsing
  bubbles in general). Now: "Collapsing bubbles like these can even flash with light, too faint to see." Sparks are
  tagged as an illustration.
- Holds: one of the fastest strikes (trap-jaw ants are faster; "one of" covers it), vapour bubbles (2004), two force
  peaks per strike ~0.4-0.5 ms apart, the second from bubble collapse (2005), O. scyllarus smashes snails (2005 Methods).
- Hook kept as the planner wrote it (loose: the bubbles form in the gap at the shell; the picture puts them there).
- Sources: https://doi.org/10.1038/428819a ; https://doi.org/10.1242/jeb.01831 ; https://doi.org/10.1038/35095152
