Most people think your brain switches off when you sleep.
But when you dream, parts of it are almost as busy as when you're awake.
Deep inside, the brainstem flips on dream sleep.
It makes your eyes dart around. And it keeps your body still, so you don't act out your dream.
At the back, the seeing part makes pictures, even with your eyes shut. That's how he can fly over his town.
The feelings part turns way up. That's why a dream can feel scary or exciting.
But the front part, the one that asks "does this make sense?", goes quiet. So flying feels normal.
The memory part mixes bits of today with old memories.
All of this is tiny brain cells talking. One cell sends a spark down its long arm. At the end, it sends chemicals across a tiny gap to the next cell.
So the brainstem holds you still, the back makes pictures, the feelings turn up, and the front lets it all happen.
Then the alarm rings, and most of the dream slips away.
