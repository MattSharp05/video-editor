# Dream brain 01: script v0.2 + beat sheet (draft for Matthew)

**Idea:** When you dream, your brain doesn't switch off: different parts do different jobs that build the dream, down
to cells passing sparks. **Goal:** reach. **Format:** Reel 9:16, ~68 s, one continuous camera move, luminous 3D (style A).
**Voice:** AI American male (design-m2, cloned from the approved clip). No call to action.
Lines: `lines_v02.txt` (191 words, Flesch-Kincaid grade 2.7). Fact-check notes in `brief.md`.

**Thread:** his town and his brain are both lit only by windows, streets and streetlights. The town appears at three
scales: the real town at night, a copy the dream builds out of light inside his head, and one street that becomes a
nerve cell. Colour: gold = dream/signal (the thing to follow), red = feelings, cool blue = resting brain, grey = quiet.

| # | Spoken line | What the viewer sees and what happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Most people think your brain switches off when you sleep. | High above his small town at night in fog. Windows go dark one by one; one stays lit. We drop through it: a man asleep, a faint glow under the skin of his head, like light under a door. |
| 2 | But when you dream, parts of it are almost as busy as when you're awake. | We push through his temple: inside, the brain is a city at night, light trails running along the folds like rush hour at 3 a.m. Small label: AWAKE / DREAMING bars, nearly equal. |
| 3 | Deep inside, the brainstem switches on a kind of sleep called REM. That's when most big, vivid dreams happen. | Sink to the brainstem, a glowing stalk. It throbs once; a wave of light climbs and the brain brightens floor by floor. Tag: "REM". |
| 4 | It makes your eyes dart around. And it keeps most of your muscles still, so you don't act out your dream. | The light splits: one branch to the backs of his eyeballs, which flick left-right together. We tilt down the spinal cord: pulses for the body hit a glowing barrier and fizzle; his ghostly body below stays dark. |
| 5 | At the back, the seeing parts make pictures, even with your eyes shut. That's how he can fly over his town. | The low back and sides glow and spray particles that build his town, block by block, as a glowing model in the middle of the brain. We dive in and it's full size: we fly behind a glowing figure, arms out. |
| 6 | The feelings part turns up. That's why a dream can feel scary or exciting. | Deep below, the almond-shaped feelings part pulses red like a heartbeat; red floods the dream sky. A gust drops him to the rooftops (scary), then he swoops past the moon (exciting). |
| 7 | But the front part, the one that asks "does this make sense?", gets very quiet. So flying feels normal. | The front hangs over the dream like a lighthouse; its searchlight makes an odd thing (a bus in a cloud) blink out. Then it dims to grey, the light dies, and odd things drift by and stay. He glides past. |
| 8 | The memory parts mix bits of today with older memories. | Two ribbons stream in from the memory area: cool white (today: his office, a coffee cup) and warm amber (the past: his childhood house, old school). Buildings swap places in the town. |
| 9 | All of this is tiny brain cells talking. One cell sends a spark down its long arm. At the end, it sends chemicals across a tiny gap to the next cell. | Dive at one streetlight on the main street: the row of lights becomes a nerve cell. A spark races down its arm like streetlights coming on; at the tip glowing dots (the dream's fireflies) cross a tiny gap and dock; the next cell lights. |
| 10 | So the brainstem holds you still, the back makes pictures, the feelings turn up, and the front stops asking questions. | Fast pull-out: gap, cell, network, whole brain, dream model glowing at the centre. Each region pulses as it's named and its effect flares again. |
| 11 | Then the alarm rings, and most of the dream slips away. | The bedside clock rings; rings of sound ripple in. The brain snaps to even white "awake" light; the dream town crumbles from the edges until only the bus is left, then it goes. Pull out through the window: his town's windows switch on for morning (mirrors beat 1). |

## Prototype first (riskiest)
Beat 4 inside view (eyes + still body must read in ~6 s), beat 9 street-to-neuron morph, beat 8 today vs past
legible with sound off.
